\begin{abstract}
I study the $(\infty, n)$-categorical moduli space $\fM$ of formal systems (hereafter \textbf{MSFS}): the classifying $2$-stack of Morita-equivalence classes of Rich-foundations ($\LFnd$ theories satisfying (R1)--(R5)). I distinguish structural constructions, conditional comparison criteria, open stabilization obligations, and a relative boundary lemma.

\emph{(i) Conditional plurality at $\LCls$.} Classifiers over a common base with distinct essential classification images cannot be equivalent over that base. Applying this criterion to $\infty$-cosmoi, Univalent Foundations, and cohesive frameworks requires explicit classifiers and verification of their images.

\emph{(ii) Conditional meta-categoricity.} Comparison with a common classifier gives a biequivalence when essential surjectivity and equivalences on all hom-categories are supplied. Deriving these lifting conditions from (Max-1)--(Max-4) remains open.

\emph{(iii) Conditional intensional refinement.} A supplied compatible comparison makes a refinement fibre over its chosen base image. An ordinary slice construction exists, but its equivalence-generated display filtration collapses at grade zero. A nontrivial higher refinement and strict Morita separation remain construction problems.

\emph{(iv) Meta-stabilization problem.} Equivalence between iterated classifiers is not established. Fixed-finite-$n$ unicity of higher-category models does not supply it, and unbounded universe ascent requires additional assumptions.

\emph{(v) Two-stage AC/OC duality.} Before localization, the section--forgetful adjunction $\varepsilon \dashv \alpha$ between theories and pointed reflective enactments is strictly asymmetric ($\alpha \circ \varepsilon = \id_\cF$, while each enactment fibre $\alpha^{-1}(F)$ retains distinct semantic realizations and resource budgets). At a specified categorical level, when the required localizations and descent data exist, inverting the reflection morphisms yields a coarse equivalence $\fM \simeq \fM_\cE$. Independently of localization, the Dual Boundary Lemma $\LAbs^\cE = \emptyset$ holds directly in $\cE$.

\emph{Boundary corollary: the Absolute Foundation No-Go Theorem (AFN-T).} The defined $\LAbs$ stratum (formally definable, non-reducible, and maximally generative foundation) is empty --- both via the direct/slice-conservative identity mechanism $(\alpha_0)$ across all $S \in \RS$ and, for arbitrary faithful subtheory inclusions $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$, via strong diagonal anti-absoluteness $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ under a canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration and classical (or negative-translated) relative interpretability. AFN-T stands alongside the classical no-go series Cantor~\cite{Cantor1891}, Russell~\cite{Russell1903}, G\"odel~\cite{Godel1931}, Tarski~\cite{Tarski1936}, Lawvere~\cite{Lawvere1969}, Ernst~\cite{Ernst2015} as a categorical boundary schema (without reproving their domain-specific diagonals) and extends dually to the enactment-absolute stratum $\LAbs^\cE$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} moduli space of formal systems, classifying $2$-stacks, categorical logic, $(\infty, n)$-categories, higher topos theory, intensional type theory, meta-classification, effective topos, no-go theorems.

\noindent\textbf{MSC2020:} 03A05, 03B30, 03C45, 03F40, 18A05, 18N10, 18N50, 18N60, 18N65.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{Scope of the obstruction.}
The identity argument $(\alpha_0)$ of AFN-T is relative to the defined class $\cS_S$ and the definition of $\Pi_4$, while the diagonal argument $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ rules out internal interpretability of all Rich-foundations inside any formally $S$-presented candidate under canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration and classical (or negative-translated) proof-translation. Neither mechanism excludes new mathematics, new foundations, or operational refinements of Morita-equivalent realizations. In particular, a cost or time invariant descends through a localization only if it sends every inverted morphism to an equivalence. The reflective localization used for enactments does not automatically retain execution cost, causality, memory, or production resources; any resource-sensitive realization therefore retains those data \emph{above} the localized classifier and requires separate proofs of preservation.

This paper studies the \emph{moduli space of formal systems} $\fM$, the classifying $(\infty, 2)$-stack whose points are Morita-equivalence classes of Rich-foundations (formal theories $F$ satisfying conditions (R1)--(R5) of Definition~\ref{def:rs}) and whose $k$-morphisms are faithful interpretations and provable natural equivalences. The principal results concern the structure of $\fM$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stratification} (\S\ref{sec:hierarchy}): $\LFnd \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Cls}} \LCls \supseteq \LClsMax \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Gen}} \LAbs = \emptyset$ --- the strata are cut out by type-distinct condition packages ((R1)--(R5) on formal systems vs.\ (M1)--(M5) on classifiers, Proposition~\ref{prop:level-structure}~(ii)); the sole inclusion is $\LClsMax \subseteq \LCls$.
\item \textbf{Conditional structural plurality at $\LCls$} (Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-mult}).
\item \textbf{Conditional categoricity of $\LClsMax$} (Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-cat}).
\item \textbf{Open meta-stabilization and universe obligations} (Remark~\ref{thm:meta-stab}).
\item \textbf{Conditional slice-locality} (Theorem~\ref{thm:slice-locality}), requiring a supplied compatible comparison.
\item \textbf{Generated filtrations and their limitation} (Definition~\ref{def:display-grade}, Proposition~\ref{prop:grading}): generator-preserving functors cannot increase grade; an equivalence-only seed gives grade zero everywhere (Corollary~\ref{cor:grade-collapse}).
\item \textbf{Five-axis analysis} (\S\ref{sec:five-axis}).
\item \textbf{Two-stage AC/OC comparison} (Theorem~\ref{thm:ac-oc-duality}) and the \textbf{Dual Boundary Lemma} $\LAbsE = \emptyset$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dual-afnt}).
\item \textbf{Outer boundary: $\LAbs = \emptyset$} --- the Absolute Foundation No-Go Theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}, Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-beta}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Machine verification.} Alongside the Verum MSFS corpus (combining Wave~1 finite/dependent proofs with quarantined legacy schematic files), the companion constructive Lean~4 kernel (\texttt{lean4-mf-kernel}, zero \texttt{Classical.choice}, zero \texttt{sorry}) verifies strict $2$-categorical Pronk fractions, non-thin display-grade tightness, $13$-axiom interface models, provenance/Clock-Trap translation adequacy, and end-to-end constructive reconstructions; full audit counts, replay commands, and translation-adequacy tiers appear in Appendix~\ref{app:verification-ledger}. The current paper does not assert machine-checked coverage of every prose statement or open higher-categorical construction.

\subsection{Notation and Conventions}

\begin{convention}\label{conv:zfc-inacc}
I work throughout in $\ZFC$ augmented by two inaccessible cardinals $\kappa_1 < \kappa_2$, providing Grothendieck universes $\mathbf{U}_1 \subset \mathbf{U}_2$ sufficient for the $2$-categorical machinery used. The categorical machinery follows Kelly~\cite{Kelly1982} for enriched and $2$-categories, Lurie~\cite{LurieHTT} for $(\infty, 1)$-categories, Lurie~\cite{LurieHA} for higher algebra, and Riehl--Verity~\cite{RiehlVerity2022} for $\infty$-cosmoi. For accessible functor theory I follow Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994}.
\end{convention}

\begin{convention}[Key symbols]\label{conv:notation}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\cF$ denotes the $2$-category of \emph{Rich-foundations} ($\LFnd$): objects are foundational theories in the sense formalised in \S\ref{sec:rs} below (conditions (R1)--(R5)); $1$-morphisms are faithful interpretations; $2$-morphisms are provable natural equivalences between interpretations.
\item $\rho$ denotes the \emph{classification assignment} sending each $F \in \cF$ (with its intrinsic non-invertible depth $n_F$, Definition~\ref{def:rs}) to its category of models $\rho_{n_F}(F) \in (\infty, n_F)\text{-}\Cat$ (or, for type-theoretic $F$, its syntactic $(\infty, n_F)$-category $\Syn(F)$). Whenever foundations are compared at a common comparison depth $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, $\rho$ denotes the induced assignment into $(\infty, n)\text{-}\Cat$ via explicitly supplied depth-comparison functors (cf.\ the truncation clause below); otherwise $\rho$ is typed fiberwise over $F \mapsto n_F$.
\item A \emph{gauge transformation} $\tau : F_1 \to F_2$ is a $1$-morphism in $\cF$ accompanied by a distinguished $2$-equivalence $\rho(F_1) \simeq \rho(F_2)$ at the model-categorical level. Two foundations are \emph{gauge-equivalent} if there is an invertible gauge transformation between them. \emph{Relation to Morita equivalence.} Gauge-equivalence at the $\cF$-level corresponds, via the $\fM := \cF[\mathrm{gauge}^{-1}]$ localization, to Morita equivalence at the moduli level: $F_1, F_2 \in \cF$ are gauge-equivalent iff $[F_1] = [F_2]$ in $\fM$, which by construction is the Morita-equivalence class. Throughout the paper, ``gauge'' ($1$-morphism level) and ``Morita'' (moduli level) name the same underlying equivalence relation at their respective categorical strata, related by the localization functor $\cF \to \fM$; no distinct third relation is introduced.
\item \emph{Morita-reducibility at the $(\infty, n)$-level}: a $\LAbs$ candidate $X$ and any $Y \in \cS_S$ are each viewed as pointed $(\infty, n)$-categorical structures via the pointing construction in the total $(\infty, n)$-category $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ of $S$-structured $(\infty, n)$-categories (Definition~\ref{def:strcat}, stated immediately below). Concretely, $X$ presented by a formula $\phi_X$ corresponds to the pointed pair $(\Syn(S), [\phi_X])$ where $[\phi_X]$ is the isomorphism class of the object defined by $\phi_X$ in the syntactic category; $Y \in \Ob(\cM) \subseteq \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$ corresponds to $(\cM, Y)$. I write $X \to_{M, n} Y$, and say $X$ is \emph{Morita-reducible at the $(\infty, n)$-level} to $Y$, if there exists an $S$-preserving pointed $(\infty, n)$-functor $\Phi : (\Syn(S), [\phi_X]) \to (\cM, Y)$ in $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ sending $[\phi_X]$ to $Y$ (up to equivalence) such that the induced functor on pointed slices $\Phi/[\phi_X] : \Syn(S)/[\phi_X] \to \cM/Y$ (equivalently, on the full pointed sub-$(\infty, n)$-category generated by $[\phi_X]$, or on the whole pointed ambient structure when comparing whole contexts) is an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence onto its essential image. This extends classical ring-Morita equivalence (which compares $\Mod(X)$ and $\Mod(Y)$ as $1$-categories) by incorporating the full $(\infty, n)$-categorical structure; the two notions coincide when $X, Y$ are $1$-categorical and $n = 1$. I use the shorthand ``Morita-equivalent'' for the symmetric notion and ``Morita-reducible'' for the directed version.
\item $\fM$ denotes the \emph{classifying $2$-groupoid / prestack} of $\cF$: the localization $\fM := \cF[\mathrm{gauge}^{-1}]$ of Rich-foundations at gauge transformations (informally called the classifying $2$-stack when equipped with descent data). No Grothendieck topology or site is postulated on $\fM$ unless explicitly supplied for a given construction. When a family of foundations is fibered over a base category $\mathcal{B}$ by a pseudofunctor $F : \mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \Cat$, the Grothendieck construction $\int F \to \mathcal{B}$ (Lurie HTT~\cite{LurieHTT} \S 3.2, straightening--unstraightening of Cartesian fibrations) is strictly distinguished from the literal point-preimage $\rho^{-1}(S)$ of the classification functor $\rho$, which does not by itself define a Cartesian fibration over $\RS$ without specified base functor and Cartesian lifts.
\item For a $2$-category $\mathbf{F}$, $\mathrm{Aut}_2(\mathbf{F})$ denotes the $2$-group of $2$-autoequivalences $\mathbf{F} \xrightarrow{\simeq_2} \mathbf{F}$ (monoidal under composition, with invertible $1$-morphisms and coherent $2$-cells).
\item $\Sint$ denotes the $2$-category of display $2$-categories (rigorous definition deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:categorical} below).
\item All $2$-categories are locally small and $\kappa_1$-accessible unless stated otherwise. (For specific Rich-foundations $S$ whose consistency strength requires the second inaccessible, the $\Syn(S)$-accessibility parameter generalizes to $\kappa_S \in \{\kappa_1, \kappa_2\}$ per the accessibility clause (R5a) of the Rich-metatheory definition, formalised in \S\ref{sec:rs} below; subsequent $\kappa_1$-statements should be read uniformly as $\kappa_S$-statements where $S$ is indexed by the specific $\LFnd$-point under consideration. All proofs in this paper are stable under this uniform reading, since the accessibility arguments invoke only regularity of the cardinal, not its specific value.)
\item \emph{Truncation and comparison across depths}: $\Syn^{(\infty,n)}(S)$ denotes a presentation in an explicitly chosen model at depth $n$, when such a presentation and its comparison maps have been supplied. Changing depth requires functors with stated universal properties and coherent transition maps; a reference to left Kan extension alone does not construct a lift of a single syntactic category. The notation $n=\infty$ requires a separately specified unbounded-depth model. No equality of this model with an unspecified colimit, or stabilization under enrichment, is assumed (see Theorems~\ref{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity} and~\ref{thm:cond-infty-stab} and Remark~\ref{thm:bergner-lurie-stab}).
\end{itemize}
\end{convention}

\begin{definition}[The total $(\infty, n)$-category $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$]\label{def:strcat}
For $S \in \RS$ and $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, the \emph{total $(\infty, n)$-category of $S$-structured $(\infty, n)$-categories}, denoted $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$, has:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objects}: pairs $(\cC, \mathrm{str}_\cC)$ where $\cC$ is a $\mathbf{U}_2$-small $(\infty, n)$-category that is either essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small or $\kappa_S$-accessible within $\mathbf{U}_2$ (covering syntactic categories and model categories respectively; cf.\ (R5a)) and $\mathrm{str}_\cC$ is an $S$-model structure on $\cC$ (i.e., an interpretation of $S$-signature into $\cC$ satisfying the axioms of $S$).
\item \textbf{$1$-morphisms}: $S$-preserving $(\infty, n)$-functors $\Phi : (\cC_1, \mathrm{str}_1) \to (\cC_2, \mathrm{str}_2)$, i.e., functors commuting with the respective $S$-model structures up to coherent $(\infty, n)$-isomorphism.
\item \textbf{Higher morphisms}: coherent $(\infty, n)$-natural transformations between $S$-preserving functors.
\end{itemize}
I embed objects of $\Syn(S)$ and of any model $\cM \in \mathbf{U}_2$ of $S$ into $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ via the pointing construction $X \mapsto (\Syn(S), X)$ and $Y \mapsto (\cM, Y)$ respectively. Morita-reducibility, as specified in Convention~\ref{conv:notation}, refers to the existence of an $S$-preserving $(\infty, n)$-functor in $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ between such pointed structures that is an equivalence onto its image.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Assumption Register}\label{sec:assumption-register}

The register distinguishes the metatheoretic base, structural definitions, and additional hypotheses of conditional results. In particular, the comparison criterion does not derive categoricity from (Max-1)--(Max-4), and the proposed meta-stabilization is open. Downstream uses must check the hypotheses of the cited statement rather than infer proved status from a theorem number.

\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{(A1) Foundational base.}] $\ZFC$ plus two strongly inaccessible cardinals $\kappa_1<\kappa_2$ (Convention~\ref{conv:zfc-inacc}). \emph{Status: foundational axiom package.} This is the declared base for constructions whose size and existence conditions have been verified. It does not certify its own consistency. A model construction gives an upper consistency bound; equiconsistency requires a converse interpretation or a separate lower-bound argument.
\item[\textbf{(A2) Scope of ``metatheory''.}] The no-go quantifies over $\RS$ as delimited by (R1)--(R5) (\S\ref{sec:rs}): recursively enumerable, interpreting Robinson's $\mathsf{Q}$, possessing a model in $\mathbf{U}_2$, $\mathbf{StrCat}$-compatible, with essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small $\Syn(S)$ of $\kappa_S$-accessible $\mathrm{Ind}$-completion (R5a). \emph{Status: definitional scope.} Systems outside $\RS$ --- non-r.e.\ oracle theories, infinitary logics without accessible syntax --- are not ruled on. Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} applies directly across $\RS$ for direct/slice-conservative presentations $(\alpha_0)$, and for arbitrary faithful subtheories $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ under a canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration and classical (or negative-translated) relative interpretability.
\item[\textbf{(A3) Formal content of ``absolute foundation''.}] $\LAbs$ membership is \emph{defined} as the conjunction $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S, n}) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ (Definition~\ref{def:level6}). \emph{Status: interpretive adequacy assumption.} AFN-T forecloses precisely this triple; the philosophical reading ``no absolute foundation exists'' is faithful exactly to the extent the triple captures the intended notion. The five-axis analysis of \S\ref{sec:five-axis} hardens this reading axis-by-axis but does not eliminate its interpretive character.
\item[\textbf{(A4) Comparison relation.}] Objects are compared through Morita-reducibility: existence of an $S$-preserving $(\infty, n)$-functor in $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ that is an equivalence onto its image, with objects embedded by the pointing construction (Definition~\ref{def:strcat}). \emph{Status: structural convention.} A finer or coarser comparison topology would redraw stratum boundaries; \S\ref{sec:intensional-grading} measures exactly what the Morita quotient forgets.
\item[\textbf{(A5) Axis exhaustiveness.}] The escape-parameter space is taken to be the five axes $(S, n, \mu, \xi, \pi)$ of \S\ref{sec:five-axis}. \emph{Status: definitional scope, not a theorem.} Exhaustiveness holds by definition of what the paper counts as an escape parameter; a genuinely new axis would require a new theorem, not contradict an existing one.
\item[\textbf{(A6) Meta-classifier axioms.}] The proposed classifying apparatus satisfies (M1)--(M5) and, for the maximal class, (Max-1)--(Max-4). \emph{Status: axioms on a proposed classifier.} The joint-separation clause of (Max-4) supplies neither morphism lifts nor equivalences on hom-categories. Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-cat} additionally requires comparison functors that are essentially surjective and locally equivalences. Existence of these data remains open.
\item[\textbf{(A7) Localization semantics of equivalence.}] The AC/OC comparison (\S\ref{sec:ac-oc-duality}) reads coarse ``equivalence'' through $\mathrm{gauge}_\cE$, the $2$-localization of $\cE$ at the base-gauge reflection morphisms $\mathcal{R}_\cE := \alpha^{-1}(\mathrm{gauge}_\cF)$ (Pronk calculus of fractions, (BF1)--(BF5)): both the pseudonatural counit $(\id_F, \iota) : \varepsilon(F) \to E$ of $\varepsilon \dashv \alpha$ and the per-object retraction $(\id_F, r) : E \to \varepsilon(F)$ lie in $\mathcal{R}_\cE$ and become mutually quasi-inverse \emph{after} localization, never in $\cE$ itself, where the internal reflector unit $\eta_r : \id_\cC \Rightarrow \iota \circ r$ remains non-invertible. \emph{Status: semantic convention}, of the same kind as ring-theoretic Morita equivalence.
\item[\textbf{(A8) Display generators.}] A filtration requires specified seeds and allowed operations. \emph{Status: presentation data.} The formerly canonical seed of equivalences produces a constant filtration. Nontrivial syntactic or resource-sensitive generators and invariance under their comparison maps are additional obligations.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[Interface for limiting extensions]\label{rem:register-interface}
The register is an interface for extensions: each extension must specify which assumptions it inherits and which it replaces. An extension may require a stronger base; neither an upper bound nor equiconsistency follows from naming the base. AFN-T excludes the explicitly defined $\LAbs$ conjunction within its stated definability scope. It does not prove consistency of other proposed extensions, categoricity of a maximal classifier, or a bound on future mathematical constructions.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Formal Strata}

\begin{definition}[Stratified hierarchy --- formal]\label{def:hierarchy}
I define the formal \emph{strata} as classes of mathematical objects:

\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Class & Membership criterion \\ \midrule
$\LFnd$ & Formal systems satisfying (R1)--(R5) (specified in \S\ref{sec:rs} below) \\
$\LCls$ & Classifiers satisfying (M1)--(M5) (specified in \S\ref{sec:meta} below) \\
$\LClsMax$ & Classifiers additionally satisfying (Max-1)--(Max-4) (specified in \S\ref{sec:meta} below) \\
$\LAbs$ & Objects satisfying $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S, n}) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ for some $(S, n)$ (specified in \S\ref{sec:conditions} below) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Structural properties of the hierarchy]\label{prop:level-structure}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{(i)}] \emph{Definability.} For every fixed finite depth $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (and for $n = \infty$ once a specific unbounded-depth model of $(\infty, \infty)$-categories has been fixed in $\mathbf{U}_2$, per Convention~\ref{conv:notation}), each of the four strata is a (possibly proper) class definable in $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$ augmented by the $2$-categorical machinery of Convention~\ref{conv:zfc-inacc}. For $\LFnd$ the conditions are (R1)--(R5) (\S\ref{sec:rs} below); for $\LCls$ they are (M1)--(M5) (\S\ref{sec:meta} below); for $\LClsMax$ they strengthen to (Max-1)--(Max-4) (\S\ref{sec:meta} below); $\LAbs$ is defined by the triple $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S, n}) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ for some $(S, n)$ (\S\ref{sec:conditions} below; the structural self-contradiction of this triple, recorded in \S\ref{sec:afnt-alpha}, yields Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}).
\item[\textbf{(ii)}] \emph{Multi-stratum membership.} $\LFnd$ and $\LCls$ concern different data: a formal theory and a specified classifier. A constructed object carrying both structures can satisfy both condition packages. No such membership is inferred solely from the name of a research programme.
\item[\textbf{(iii)}] \emph{Inclusion at the maximal layer.} $\LClsMax\subseteq\LCls$. Strictness requires a constructed classifier failing at least one maximality condition. Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-cat} is a conditional biequivalence criterion with additional comparison data, and Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-mult} gives a conditional image-separation criterion; neither constructs the proposed witnesses.
\item[\textbf{(iv)}] \emph{Emptiness of the absolute stratum.} $\LAbs = \emptyset$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}. Consequently $\LFnd \cap \LAbs = \LCls \cap \LAbs = \LClsMax \cap \LAbs = \emptyset$ trivially.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{(i)} For every fixed finite $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (and for $n = \infty$ relative to a separately specified unbounded-depth model in $\mathbf{U}_2$, matching Convention~\ref{conv:notation}), each membership criterion is first-order expressible in $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$; hence the specified classes are definable.

\textbf{(ii)} The condition sets concern disjoint aspects of an object: axioms with proof calculus vs.\ $2$-categorical structure with classifying functor. An object carrying both structures satisfies both criteria simultaneously.

\textbf{(iii)} Inclusion follows because (Max-1)--(Max-4) strengthen (M1)--(M5). Neither strictness nor equivalence of maximal classifiers follows from this implication alone.

\textbf{(iv)} Immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Two Meta-Operations}\label{sec:indexing-scheme}

\paragraph{Horizontal and vertical meta.} Two categorically distinct meta-operations act on a stratum $\mathcal{L}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Horizontal (classifying) meta.} $\mathrm{Cls}(\mathcal{L})$ is the class of $2$-categories $\mathbf{F}$ \emph{classifying} but not \emph{generating} members of $\mathcal{L}$: locally small, equipped with an accessible classification functor into the moduli of $\mathcal{L}$, non-generative, and metatheory-parametrized (cf.\ (M1)--(M5)).
\item \textbf{Vertical (generative) meta.} $\mathrm{Gen}(\mathcal{L})$ is the class of frameworks \emph{generating} members of $\mathcal{L}$ as internal derivations --- candidate absolute foundations.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Non-collapse of the horizontal meta at $\LFnd$]\label{prop:no-collapse}
The horizontal classifier stratum $\LCls := \mathrm{Cls}(\LFnd)$ is neither a subclass of $\LFnd$ ($\LCls \not\subseteq \LFnd$) nor contained in or equal to $\LAbs$. It is therefore a distinct formal stratum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch.]
The classifier package (M1)--(M5) specifies a locally small $2$-category $\mathbf{F}$ equipped with a classification functor $\Cl_{\mathbf{F}} : \mathbf{F} \to \fM$ and accessible reflection $R_{\mathbf{F}}$, which does not by itself supply a formal theory satisfying (R1)--(R5). While a composite structure carrying both packages can satisfy both criteria simultaneously (Proposition~\ref{prop:level-structure}~(ii)), satisfaction of (M1)--(M5) alone does not imply membership in $\LFnd$, so $\LCls \not\subseteq \LFnd$. Furthermore, (M4) requires non-generativity whereas $\LAbs$ requires maximal generativity $(\Pi_{3\text{-max}})$ (and is empty by Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}), so $\LCls$ does not coincide with $\LAbs$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Horizontal stabilization.} Equivalence of iterated classifiers is an open problem (Remark~\ref{thm:meta-stab}); AFN-T does not prove that no further classifier structure can arise.

\paragraph{Vertical step to the forbidden stratum.} $\LAbs \supseteq \mathrm{Gen}(\LFnd)$: a member of $\LAbs$ would be a maximally-generative foundation from which all of $\LFnd$ derives. This is categorically a fresh operation, not an iteration of $\mathrm{Cls}$. By AFN-T, $\LAbs = \emptyset$.

\paragraph{Summary.} The formal sequence reads:
\[
\underbrace{\LFnd}_{\text{(R1)--(R5)}} \; \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Cls}} \; \underbrace{\LCls}_{\text{(M1)--(M5)}} \; \supseteq \; \underbrace{\LClsMax}_{(\mathrm{Max}\text{-1})\text{--}(\mathrm{Max}\text{-4})} \; \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Gen}} \; \underbrace{\LAbs = \emptyset}_{\text{AFN-T}}
\]

\section{Reasonable Rich-Metatheories}\label{sec:rs}

\subsection{Rich-Metatheory Conditions}

\begin{definition}[Reasonable Rich-Metatheory]\label{def:rs}
A theory $S$ formulated in a first-order meta-syntax is a \textbf{reasonable Rich-metatheory} (or \textbf{R-S}) if it satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(R1) Arithmetic completeness}: $S$ interprets Robinson arithmetic $\mathsf{Q}$. This defines the baseline class $\RS = \RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ (which includes bounded-arithmetic systems such as $\mathsf{I\Delta_0}, \mathsf{S}_2^i, \mathsf{T}_2^i$ as well as $\mathrm{EFA} = \mathsf{I\Delta_0} + \exp$). When full Peano induction is additionally required (so that $S$ interprets $\PA$), we write $\RS_{\PA} \subsetneq \RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$; see Boundary cases below and Question~(Q5).
\item \textbf{(R2) Recursive enumerability}: the set of axioms of $S$ is recursively enumerable.
\item \textbf{(R3) Model existence}: $S$ has at least one model $\cM$ in the ambient set-theoretic meta-universe $\mathbf{U}_2$ (the outer Grothendieck universe of Convention~\ref{conv:zfc-inacc}, large enough to accommodate models of all listed R-S examples including $\ZFC$ + inaccessible cardinals, whose smallest models have size $\kappa_1$ and therefore lie in $\mathbf{U}_2 \setminus \mathbf{U}_1$).
\item \textbf{(R4) G\"odel coding}: there exists a recursive injection $\ulcorner \cdot \urcorner : \mathrm{Formulas}(S) \to \mathbb{N}$ for which the predicates ``$n$ is the G\"odel number of a provable formula'' and ``$n$ is the G\"odel number of a well-formed formula'' are $S$-definable. (For any countable recursive first-order signature, (R4) follows classically from (R1)+(R2)+(R3); see Proposition~\ref{prop:rs-independence}.)
\item \textbf{(R5) Categorical semantics}: there exists $n_S \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, the \emph{intrinsic non-invertible categorical depth} of $S$ (all $k$-morphisms for $k > n_S$ are invertible, matching Appendix~\ref{app:categorical}), together with a \emph{homotopy truncation level} $h_S \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, such that
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(R5a)}] the syntactic category $\Syn(S)$ of $S$ (objects: contexts; $1$-morphisms: provable-from-context substitutions; higher morphisms: proofs of equality of substitutions) is an essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small $(\infty, n_S)$-category whose category of models $\Mod(S) \simeq \Fun^{\mathrm{lex}}(\Syn(S), -)$ is $\kappa_S$-accessible --- equivalently, whose $\mathrm{Ind}_{\kappa_S}$-completion $\mathrm{Ind}_{\kappa_S}(\Syn(S))$ is a $\kappa_S$-accessible $(\infty, n_S)$-category (Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994} Thm.~2.26; all accessibility statements about $\Syn(S)$ below are to be read in this $\mathrm{Ind}$-completed sense, while size statements refer to $\Syn(S)$ itself) --- where $\kappa_S \in \{\kappa_1, \kappa_2\}$ is determined by $S$: for $S$ of consistency strength $\leq \kappa_1$ (e.g., $\ZFC$, $\PA$, $\HoTT$, $\MLTT$, $\CIC$) set $\kappa_S = \kappa_1$; for $S$ of consistency strength requiring the second inaccessible (e.g., $\ZFC$ + inaccessible) set $\kappa_S = \kappa_2$. The accessibility parameter follows the theory's consistency strength; all theorems below are stated uniformly for arbitrary $\kappa_S \in \{\kappa_1, \kappa_2\}$ unless a specific case is flagged;
\item[\textbf{(R5b)}] the syntax-to-semantics evaluation $\ev_\cM : \Syn(S) \to \cM$ for every model $\cM \models S$ is an $S$-preserving $(\infty, n_S)$-functor, and the syntactic-category / internal-language pair $(\Syn, \mathrm{Lang})$ constitutes a Lambek--Scott-type adjunction $\Syn \dashv \mathrm{Lang}$ between theories and $S$-structured $(\infty, n_S)$-categories (with the corresponding contravariant syntax--semantics duality $\mathrm{Th} \dashv \Mod^{\mathrm{op}}$ assigning $\Mod(T) \simeq \Fun^{\mathrm{str}}(\Syn(T), -)$), whose unit at $S$ exhibits $\Syn(S)$ as the \emph{initial $S$-structured $(\infty, n_S)$-category} in the $(\infty, n_S + 1)$-category of $S$-structured $(\infty, n_S)$-categories and $S$-preserving $(\infty, n_S)$-functors.
\end{itemize}
The syntax--semantics data of (R5a)--(R5b) are required hypotheses on each theory and chosen categorical model. Lambek--Scott~\cite{LambekScott1986} treats ordinary categorical logic ($\Syn \dashv \mathrm{Lang}$), while the cited homotopy-theoretic works provide particular type-theoretic semantics. Finite-depth model comparison~\cite{BarwickSchommerPries2021,BergnerRezk2013} does not by itself construct a syntax--semantics adjunction for every formal theory or every depth. Each use must supply the required functoriality and universal property.
\emph{Note on non-invertible depth $n_S$ vs.\ homotopy truncation level $h_S$.} Consistent with Appendix~\ref{app:categorical}, $n_S$ denotes the highest dimension of \emph{non-invertible} morphisms in $\Syn(S)$ (all $k$-morphisms for $k > n_S$ are invertible), whereas $h_S \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ denotes the homotopy truncation level of the invertible higher-equality cells. Thus for first-order theories, $\Syn(S)$ is a $1$-category ($n_S = 1$, $h_S = 0$). For Martin-L\"of type theory ($\MLTT$), $\CIC$, and $\HoTT$ with univalence, $\Syn(S)$ is an $(\infty, 1)$-category (Kapulkin--Lumsdaine~\cite{KapulkinLumsdaine2021}, Awodey--Warren~\cite{AwodeyWarren2009}): its non-invertible morphism dimension is $n_S = 1$, while unbounded iteration of invertible identity types gives homotopy truncation level $h_S = \infty$ (strictly distinct from $(\infty, \infty)$-categories in the sense of Rezk / Barwick--Schommer-Pries~\cite{BarwickSchommerPries2021}, where non-invertible $k$-morphisms occur at every $k$). Higher non-invertible depths $n_S \ge 2$ arise only for directed higher type theories or higher-categorical syntax.

Instances verifying (R5a)--(R5b): first-order $S$ at $(n_S, h_S) = (1, 0)$ (Lambek--Scott~\cite{LambekScott1986}); Martin-L\"of type theory at $(n_S, h_S) = (1, \infty)$ (Awodey--Warren~\cite{AwodeyWarren2009}); HoTT with univalence at $(n_S, h_S) = (1, \infty)$ (Kapulkin--Lumsdaine~\cite{KapulkinLumsdaine2021}); dependent type theories (Jacobs~\cite{Jacobs1999}).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Examples of R-S}

The following are reasonable Rich-metatheories. Each entry is a \emph{formal theory} $S$ (axiomatic system with r.e.\ axiomatisation (R2) and interpreting Q (R1)); categorical structures such as topoi appear as \emph{models} of these theories rather than as theories themselves, and are cited through their internal languages.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\ZFC$; $\ZFC$ + inaccessible cardinals; $\NBG$; Morse--Kelley; $\NBG + \AFA$ (Aczel).
\item The intuitionistic type theories $\MLTT$ and $\CIC$; $\HoTT$ with univalence; universe-polymorphic $\HoTT$.
\item \emph{Linear logic with exponential} $!$, equipped with arithmetic constants sufficient to interpret Q (i.e., intuitionistic linear arithmetic, $\mathsf{ILL}_{\mathsf{PA}}$, or the bounded linear arithmetic fragment of Girard's ludics framework~\cite{Girard2001}); purely propositional linear logic does \emph{not} satisfy (R1) and is excluded.
\item The \emph{internal dependent type theory of Lurie's $\infty$-topoi} (the axiomatic presentation of an $(\infty, 1)$-topos as a model of homotopy type theory with univalent universes and higher modalities, Lurie HTT~\cite{LurieHTT} \S 5--\S 6 via the internal-language correspondence of Shulman~\cite{Shulman2019}, Awodey--Warren~\cite{AwodeyWarren2009}); the $(\infty, 1)$-topos itself is a \emph{canonical model} of this theory, not a separate theory.
\item The \emph{internal language of the Cohesive $\infty$-topos} (Schreiber~\cite{Schreiber2013}): $\HoTT$ extended by adjoint triples of modal operators $(\Pi \dashv \flat \dashv \sharp)$ corresponding to the cohesion axioms, formulated as a type-theoretic extension.
\item \emph{Motivic-extended foundations}: $\ZFC$ (or $\HoTT$) extended with axioms asserting the existence of the motivic stable $\infty$-category $\mathrm{SH}(k)$ and its A$^1$-homotopy invariance as a formal Rich-extension; the motivic $\infty$-category itself is again a model-theoretic construct, not a standalone theory.
\item The \emph{internal language of the realizability topos} $\mathrm{Eff}$: intuitionistic higher-order arithmetic with realizability (Hyland~\cite{Hyland1982}), of which $\mathrm{Eff}$ is the canonical model; we use the shorthand ``$\mathrm{Eff}$-theory'' below for this internal language.
\item \emph{Markov constructive mathematics} (Markov, Nagornyj~\cite{MarkovNagornyj1988}: $\mathsf{HA}$ + Markov's principle, with categorical semantics in $\mathrm{Eff}$).
\item \emph{Bishop constructive analysis}~\cite{Bishop1967,BridgesVita2006} (choice-free constructive real analysis with full categorical semantics in any topos with natural number object).
\item \emph{Feferman predicative analysis}~\cite{Feferman1964} ($\mathsf{ATR}_0$-strength systems with predicative comprehension).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Non-examples}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Purely propositional or contraction-free affine logic without arithmetic axioms}: fails (R1) --- without arithmetic operations and contraction/duplication sufficient to represent arbitrary numeral terms and the axioms of $\mathsf{Q}$ (in particular, totality of successor, addition, and multiplication), Robinson arithmetic $\mathsf{Q}$ is not interpreted.
\item \emph{Inconsistent theories}: fail (R3).
\item \emph{Second-order arithmetic with full semantics}: fails (R2) --- the full set of valid formulas is not recursively enumerable.
\item \emph{Intuitive ``theories of everything''} in physics lacking formal axiomatization: fail (R1) and (R2).
\item \emph{Strict philosophical ultrafinitism} (Esenin-Volpin~\cite{EseninVolpin1961,EseninVolpin1970}): fails (R1) at the strictest reading --- rejecting the totality of successor, addition, or multiplication on ``infeasibly large'' arguments violates the basic totality axioms of Robinson's $\mathsf{Q}$ itself (already without invoking any induction schema). Bounded arithmetic variants (see Boundary cases below) behave differently.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Redundancy of (R4) over countable recursive signatures]\label{prop:rs-independence}
For any theory $S$ formulated over a countable recursive first-order signature, conditions \textup{(R1)+(R2)+(R3)} classically imply \textup{(R4)}. In particular, choosing a nonrecursive numbering does not refute the existence of an effective coding, and \textup{(R4)} is not independent of \textup{(R1)--(R3)} for countable recursive first-order presentations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every countable recursive first-order signature admits a standard primitive-recursive G\"odel coding $\ulcorner \cdot \urcorner : \mathrm{Formulas}(S) \to \mathbb{N}$ under which syntactic well-formedness is primitive recursive. By (R2), the axiom set of $S$ is recursively enumerable, so the binary proof relation $\mathrm{Proof}_S(p, n)$ is recursively enumerable ($\Sigma_1$); hence provability $\exists p\,\mathrm{Proof}_S(p, n)$ is $\Sigma_1$. By (R1), $S$ interprets Robinson arithmetic $\mathsf{Q}$, in which every $\Sigma_1$ predicate is representable (and hence $S$-definable, with consistency ensured by (R3)). Replacing $\ulcorner \cdot \urcorner$ by a nonrecursive numbering merely alters a presentation and does not destroy the existential witness (R4).
\end{proof}
Clause (R4) is therefore retained as an explicit interface condition only when abstracting over arbitrary presentations or non-standard codings. Full mutual independence of the remaining clauses (R1), (R2), (R3), (R5) requires verifying all complementary clauses in one fixed signature: in particular, since Robinson's $\mathsf{Q}$ has no induction schema, absence of Peano induction does not witness failure of (R1), and an inconsistent syntactic presentation does not automatically yield the required syntax--semantics adjunction (R5b).

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Bounded arithmetic, $\mathrm{EFA}$, and the $\RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ vs.\ $\RS_{\PA}$ bifurcation.} The bounded-arithmetic systems $\mathsf{I\Delta_0}$ and Buss's $\mathsf{S}_2^i$ / $\mathsf{T}_2^i$~\cite{Buss1986} extend Robinson's $\mathsf{Q}$ and therefore already belong to the baseline class $\RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ (satisfying (R1)--(R5) at $n_S = 1$), forming the polynomial-bounded sub-class $\RS_{\mathrm{poly}} \subsetneq \RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ (every provably total $\Sigma_1$ function is polynomially bounded by Parikh's theorem). Elementary Function Arithmetic $\mathrm{EFA} = \mathsf{I\Delta_0} + \exp$ also belongs to $\RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ and ALREADY proves totality of $2^n$; thus $\mathrm{EFA} \in \RS_{\exp} \setminus \RS_{\mathrm{poly}}$ is \emph{not} separated from $\PA$ by Parikh's polynomial bound $2^n$, but rather by the Kalm\'ar-elementary super-exponential tower $F_3 \approx H_{\omega^3}$ (proof-theoretic ordinal $\omega^3$) vs.\ $F_\alpha$ for $\alpha < \varepsilon_0$. By contrast, the super-exponential / full-induction class $\RS_{\PA} \subsetneq \RS_{\exp} \subsetneq \RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ (theories interpreting $\PA$) is strictly disjoint from $\RS_{\mathrm{poly}}$ (and hence from $\mathsf{I\Delta_0}, \mathsf{S}_2^i, \mathsf{T}_2^i$; see Question~(Q5)), while strictly weaker sub-$\mathsf{Q}$ calculi omitting total multiplication (such as $\mathsf{V}_0$) fall outside $\RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ altogether.
\item \emph{Strict ultrafinitism (Esenin-Volpin tradition).} The strictest philosophical ultrafinitism, rejecting totality of successor, addition, or multiplication for ``infeasibly large'' arguments, falls outside $\RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ at (R1) (above, Non-examples). The $\mathsf{Q}$-extending bounded-arithmetic systems in $\RS_{\mathrm{poly}} \subset \RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ provide formal reconstructions admissible into $\RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$ while remaining outside $\RS_{\PA}$.
\item \emph{Paraconsistent theories.} Appendix~\ref{app:paraconsistent} gives a conditional transfer criterion. A suitable classical kernel and a translation preserving all three boundary predicates have to be supplied.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The Class $\cS_S$}{The Class S\_S}}

\begin{definition}[$S$-definable categorical structures]\label{def:SS}
Let $S \in \RS$. Define inductively within the outer universe $\mathbf{U}_2$:
\begin{align*}
\cS_S^{\mathrm{local}} &:= \{(\cM, Y) : \cM \in \mathbf{U}_2,\ \cM \models S,\ Y \in \Ob(\cM)\} \;\cup\; \{(\Syn(S), X) : X \in \Ob(\Syn(S))\}, \\
\cS_S^{(0)} &:= \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}, \\
\cS_S^{(k+1)} &:= \cS_S^{(k)} \,\cup\, \mathcal{O}\bigl(\cS_S^{(k)}\bigr),
\end{align*}
where $\mathcal{O}$ collects the outputs (whenever the corresponding construction exists within $\mathbf{U}_2$) of the following fixed list of $(\infty, n)$-categorical operations applied to $\cS_S^{(k)}$-data:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=\textrm{(O\arabic*)}]
\item Left and right Kan extensions along $\cS_S^{(k)}$-morphisms;
\item Grothendieck constructions $\int F$ for Cartesian fibrations $F : I \to \cS_S^{(k)}$ with $I \in \cS_S^{(k)}$;
\item Limits and colimits of $\mathbf{U}_2$-small diagrams (indexed by a $\mathbf{U}_2$-small $(\infty, n)$-category $I \in \mathbf{U}_2$) valued in $\cS_S^{(k)}$;
\item Slice/comma/opposite categories built from the underlying $(\infty, n)$-categories of $\cS_S^{(k)}$-data (for opposite categories $\cM^{\mathrm{op}}$, the operation produces the dual underlying category / dual presentation without silently claiming that $\cM^{\mathrm{op}}$ inherits an $S$-model structure unless $S$ is self-dual);
\item Derived functors (Quillen sense) between $\cS_S^{(k)}$-objects equipped with model-category structure (when applicable; vacuous on objects without model structure);
\item Symmetric monoidal tensor products and internal hom in closed monoidal $\cS_S^{(k)}$-objects.
\end{enumerate}
Set $\cS_S^{\mathrm{global}} := \bigcup_{k < \omega} \cS_S^{(k)}$ and $\cS_S := \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}} \cup \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$. Every member of $\cS_S$ lies in $\mathbf{U}_2$, so $\cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$, $\cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$, and $\cS_S$ are $\mathbf{U}_2$-classes ($\subseteq \mathbf{U}_2$, of cardinality $\le \kappa_2$); restricting both the semantic base ($\cM \in \mathbf{U}_1$) and diagram indices in (O3) to $\mathbf{U}_1$ produces a $\mathbf{U}_2$-small fragment $\cS_S^{(\mathbf{U}_1)} \in \mathbf{U}_2$. Because operations such as opposite categories (O4) or arbitrary $\mathbf{U}_2$-small (co)limits do not generally preserve a fixed accessibility cardinal $\kappa_1$ or $S$-model structure, accessibility and any additional $S$-structure on derived objects in $\cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$ are checked case by case rather than assumed globally across (O1)--(O6).
\end{definition}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Key Lemma: $S$-Definability Implies $\cS_S$-Membership}{Key Lemma: S-Definability Implies S\_S-Membership}}

\begin{lemma}[$S$-definability lemma]\label{lem:SS-membership}
If $X$ is a formally $S$-definable object --- meaning $X$ is specified by a formula $\phi_X \in S$ expressing its characteristic properties --- then $X \in \cS_S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Convention~\ref{conv:notation} (Morita-reducibility clause), an object $X$ directly presented by a formula $\phi_X \in S$ is viewed in $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ as the pointed structure $(\Syn(S), [\phi_X])$. Since $[\phi_X] \in \Ob(\Syn(S))$, this pair lies in $\cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$ \emph{by the first clause of Definition~\ref{def:SS}} --- no closure operation is required. Hence $X \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}} \subseteq \cS_S$. (When $\phi_X$ is instead presented inside a proper subtheory $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$ as in Definition~\ref{def:F}, the translated formula $\iota_F(\phi_X) \in S$ defines $(\Syn(S), [\iota_F(\phi_X)]_S) \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$, whereas identifying $(\Syn(F), [\phi_X]_F)$ with $(\Syn(S), [\iota_F(\phi_X)]_S)$ as pointed $(\infty, n)$-categories requires slice-conservativity of $\Syn(\iota_F)$ --- which fails in general for non-conservative extensions such as $\PA \hookrightarrow \PA + \Con(\PA)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} below handles both the slice-conservative case and arbitrary faithful subtheories $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{Size Stratification}\label{sec:size-stratification}

\paragraph{Size classes within $\cS_S$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Small} (elements of $\mathbf{U}_1$): sets of rank $< \kappa_1$.
\item \textbf{Large} (elements of $\mathbf{U}_2 \setminus \mathbf{U}_1$): sets of rank in $[\kappa_1, \kappa_2)$.
\item \textbf{Beyond $\mathbf{U}_2$}: sets of rank $\geq \kappa_2$, including $\mathbf{U}_2$-classes (subsets of $\mathbf{U}_2$ of cardinality $\kappa_2$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cardinality bounds for ambient structures.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The $S$-definability class $\cS_S = \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}} \cup \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$ is a $\mathbf{U}_2$-class ($\cS_S \subseteq \mathbf{U}_2$, cardinality $\le \kappa_2$): its syntactic base $\{(\Syn(S), X) : X \in \Ob(\Syn(S))\}$ is essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small by (R5a), while its semantic base $\{(\cM, Y) : \cM \in \mathbf{U}_2,\ \cM \models S,\ Y \in \Ob(\cM)\}$ necessarily includes models in $\mathbf{U}_2 \setminus \mathbf{U}_1$ (as required by (R3) for theories such as $\ZFC + \text{inacc}$) and is therefore not contained in $\mathbf{U}_1$. Restricting both model carriers and (O3)-diagram indices to $\mathbf{U}_1$ yields the $\mathbf{U}_2$-small fragment $\cS_S^{(\mathbf{U}_1)} \in \mathbf{U}_2$ of cardinality $\le \kappa_1 < \kappa_2$.
\item Up to syntactic equivalence over a countable signature, there are countably many r.e.\ axiom sets, hence $|\Ob(\cF)/\!\simeq| \leq \aleph_0 \le 2^{\aleph_0} < \kappa_1$; allowing arbitrary $\mathbf{U}_2$-presentations gives $|\cF| \leq \kappa_2$.
\item For the full ambient $\mathbf{U}_2$-class $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$, $|\Ob(\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n})/\!\simeq| = \kappa_2$: indeed, for every cardinal $\mu < \kappa_2$, the product $\Set^I$ over $|I| = \mu$ (with $\mathbf{U}_1$-small sets) is a $\kappa_1$-accessible category in $\mathbf{U}_2$, yielding $\kappa_2$ pairwise non-equivalent objects. On the bounded-presentability subclass $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}^{(\mathbf{U}_1)}$ consisting of essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small objects and $\kappa_1$-accessible categories whose full subcategory of $\kappa_1$-presentable objects is essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small (Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994}, Thm.~2.26), every object is determined up to equivalence by its $\mathbf{U}_1$-small generating category and $S$-structure, giving $|\Ob(\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}^{(\mathbf{U}_1)})/\!\simeq| \leq 2^{\kappa_1} < \kappa_2$.
\item Any bound on $|\fM/\!\simeq_2|$ requires a verified size bound for its chosen presentation. Bounds for iterated classifiers require additional constructions and universe assumptions; Remark~\ref{thm:meta-stab} supplies no tower $(\kappa_k)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{The $\LAbs$ Conditions}{The L\_Abs Conditions}}\label{sec:conditions}

\begin{definition}[$(F_S)$]\label{def:F}
A pointed $(\infty, n)$-categorical structure $X$ (in the sense of Convention~\ref{conv:notation} and Definition~\ref{def:strcat}) satisfies $(F_S)$ for $S \in \RS$ iff $\exists F \in \cF$ with:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(F-1)}] $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$ (faithful relative interpretation / subtheory inclusion);
\item[\textbf{(F-2)}] $\iota_F^* : \Mod(S) \to \Mod(F)$ (induced model reduct);
\item[\textbf{(F-3)}] a formula $\phi_X$ in the language of $F$ presenting $X$ as the pointed syntactic structure $(\Syn(F), [\phi_X]_F) \simeq_{(\infty, n)} X$ (abbreviated $\Syn(F)[\phi_X]$).
\end{itemize}
The presentation is called \emph{direct} if $F = S$, and \emph{slice-conservative} if the induced pointed functor $\Syn(\iota_F) : (\Syn(F), [\phi_X]_F) \to (\Syn(S), [\iota_F(\phi_X)]_S)$ induces an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence from the slice $\Syn(F)/[\phi_X]_F$ (equivalently, from the full pointed sub-$(\infty, n)$-category generated by $[\phi_X]_F$) onto its essential image in $\Syn(S)/[\iota_F(\phi_X)]_S$ within $\mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ (for $F = S$, this holds trivially via the identity).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$(\Pi_{4, S, n})$]\label{def:pi4}
$(\Pi_{4, S, n})(X)$ holds iff $\forall Y \in \cS_S,\ \forall\ (\infty, n)\text{-functor}\ F : X \to Y$, $F$ is not an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence onto its image.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$(\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$]\label{def:pi3max}
$(\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})(X)$ holds iff for every Rich-foundation $F' \in \cF$ there exists a structure-preserving $(\infty, n)$-functor $\Syn(F') \to X$ equivalent onto its image that realizes $F'$ as an internal derivation in any foundation $F$ presenting $X$ (inducing a faithful relative interpretation $F' \to F$, parallel to the $1$-morphism $(\phi, \Phi)$ in Definition~\ref{def:level6-cE}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$\LAbs$]\label{def:level6}
$X \in \LAbs$ iff $X \models (F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S, n}) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ for some $(S, n) \in \RS \times (\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\})$.
\end{definition}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Boundary Lemma: The Emptiness of $\LAbs$ ($(\alpha)$-Part of AFN-T)}{Boundary Lemma: The Emptiness of L\_Abs ((alpha)-Part of AFN-T)}}\label{sec:afnt-alpha}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary Lemma: emptiness of $\LAbs$; AFN-T $(\alpha)$-part]\label{thm:afnt-alpha}
Let $S \in \RS$ and $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, and let $X$ be a pointed $(\infty, n)$-categorical object satisfying $(F_S)$ via $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$ and $(\Syn(F), [\phi_X]_F) \simeq_{(\infty, n)} X$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Direct / slice-conservative mechanism $(\alpha_0)$}: whenever the $(F_S)$-presentation is direct ($F = S$) or slice-conservative, already the sub-pair $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S, n})$ is inconsistent (for every $S \in \RS$ and $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, without further hypotheses).
\item \emph{Strong diagonal anti-absoluteness $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$}: suppose in addition that (a) the r.e.\ axiom set of $S$ is fixed by a canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration $\sigma_S$ in $\mathsf{Q}$ (stronger than bare (R4), determining the canonical $\Pi_1^0$ consistency sentence $\Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$), and (b) the composite interpretation $J := \iota_F \circ \tau : \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S) \to S$ induced by any witness $\tau$ of $(\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ is a classical first-order relative interpretation --- or, for intuitionistic, linear, or type-theoretic systems in $\RS$ (such as $\MLTT$, $\CIC$, $\HoTT$, $\mathsf{ILL}_{\mathsf{PA}}$, $\mathsf{HA}+\mathsf{MP}$, or topos internal languages from \S\ref{sec:rs}), is equipped with an explicit negative translation $(\cdot)^N$ of classical first-order arithmetic into $S$ together with a primitive-recursive proof-translation (verifiable in $\PA$) sending classical first-order derivations of $0 = 1$ in the arithmetic pullback to derivations of $\bot$ in $S$. Then for \emph{every} faithful subtheory inclusion $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$ (without assuming slice-conservativity of $\Syn(\iota_F)$, and even if $(\Pi_{4, S, n})$ is omitted or punctured to exclude self-equivalences), the generative pair $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ is inconsistent.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, across all $(S, n)$ in the scope of (i) or (ii), no object $X$ satisfies $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S, n}) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[$\LAbs$ emptiness]\label{cor:level6-empty-alpha}
Within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} (every direct or slice-conservative presentation over $\RS$, and every faithful subtheory presentation satisfying hypotheses (a)--(b) of $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$), $\LAbs = \emptyset$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} and Definition~\ref{def:level6}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}]
Let $X$ satisfy $(F_S)$ via $F \in \cF$, $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$, and $\phi_X \in F$ with $e : X \xrightarrow{\simeq_{(\infty, n)}} (\Syn(F), [\phi_X]_F)$.

\emph{Mechanism $(\alpha_0)$ (direct / slice-conservative case).} Suppose $F = S$ or $\Syn(\iota_F) : (\Syn(F), [\phi_X]_F) \to (\Syn(S), [\iota_F(\phi_X)]_S)$ induces an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence on the pointed slice $\Syn(F)/[\phi_X]_F$ onto its essential image in $\Syn(S)/[\iota_F(\phi_X)]_S$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership} applied to $\iota_F(\phi_X) \in S$, the target $Y := (\Syn(S), [\iota_F(\phi_X)]_S)$ lies in $\cS_S^{\mathrm{local}} \subseteq \cS_S$, and the composite pointed functor $\Syn(\iota_F) \circ e : X \to Y$ (for $F = S$, simply $\id_X : X \to X$) is an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence onto its essential image in the sense of Convention~\ref{conv:notation} with target in $\cS_S$, violating $(\Pi_{4, S, n})$.

\emph{Mechanism $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ (general faithful $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$ via diagonalization).} By (R3), $S$ possesses a model $\cM \in \mathbf{U}_2$ in the ambient metatheory $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$, so $S$ is syntactically consistent: there is no finite derivation of $\bot$ from $S$ in the standard natural numbers $\mathbb{N} = \omega \in \mathbf{U}_1$. Using the canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration $\sigma_S$ of the r.e.\ axiom set of $S$ supplied by hypothesis (a) (as opposed to an arbitrary $S$-definable provability predicate from bare (R4)), form the canonical $\Pi_1^0$ consistency sentence $\Con_{\sigma_S}(S) := \neg\mathrm{Prov}_{\sigma_S}(\ulcorner \bot \urcorner)$ and define the classical first-order arithmetic theory $T_S := \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$. Because $\mathbb{N} \models \PA$ and $\Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$ is true in $\mathbb{N}$ (no $\omega$-consistency assumption on $S$ is needed), the standard model $\mathbb{N} \in \mathbf{U}_1 \subset \mathbf{U}_2$ witnesses (R3) for $T_S$; conditions (R1), (R2), (R4), and first-order (R5) ($n_{T_S} = 1$, $\kappa_{T_S} = \kappa_1$; Lambek--Scott~\cite{LambekScott1986}) hold immediately, so $T_S \in \cF$.

Applying $(\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})(X)$ at $F' := T_S \in \cF$ yields a faithful relative interpretation $\tau : T_S \to F$. Composing with $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$ gives an interpretation $J := \iota_F \circ \tau : \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S) \to S$. Under hypothesis (b), define the classical first-order arithmetic pullback
\[
  S_{\mathrm{arith}} := \{\psi \in \mathrm{Sent}(\mathcal{L}_{\PA}) : S \vdash J(\psi)\}
\]
in the classical first-order case, or $S_{\mathrm{arith}} := \{\psi \in \mathrm{Sent}(\mathcal{L}_{\PA}) : S \vdash J(\psi^N)\}$ using the supplied negative translation $(\cdot)^N$ (for which $\Pi_1^0$ sentences such as $\Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$ are preserved) in the intuitionistic, linear, or type-theoretic case. In either case, $S_{\mathrm{arith}}$ is a consistent classical first-order recursively enumerable extension of $\PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$ whose canonical $\Sigma_1$-axiom-enumeration $\sigma_{\mathrm{arith}}$ is induced directly from $\sigma_S$ and $J$ (or $J \circ (\cdot)^N$). By hypothesis (b), every classical first-order derivation of $0 = 1$ from $S_{\mathrm{arith}}$ translates primitive recursively to an $S$-derivation of $\bot$, and since $S_{\mathrm{arith}}$ extends $\PA$ (which proves totality of primitive-recursive functions and $\Sigma_1$-collection), $S_{\mathrm{arith}} \vdash \Con_{\sigma_S}(S) \to \Con_{\sigma_{\mathrm{arith}}}(S_{\mathrm{arith}})$. Because $S_{\mathrm{arith}} \vdash \Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$, we obtain $S_{\mathrm{arith}} \vdash \Con_{\sigma_{\mathrm{arith}}}(S_{\mathrm{arith}})$ (and a fortiori a classical relative interpretation of $\mathsf{Q} + \Con_{\sigma_{\mathrm{arith}}}(S_{\mathrm{arith}})$ in $S_{\mathrm{arith}}$), contradicting the G\"odel--Feferman--Pudl\'ak Second Incompleteness Theorem (Feferman~\cite{Feferman1960}, Thm.~5.6; Pudl\'ak~\cite{Pudlak1985}, Thm.~2.1). Hence no object $X$ satisfies $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$, and a fortiori none satisfies the $\LAbs$ triple.
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Boundary Lemma: No Limit-Based Escape ($(\beta)$-Part of AFN-T)}{Boundary Lemma: No Limit-Based Escape ((beta)-Part of AFN-T)}}\label{sec:afnt-beta}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary Lemma: no limit-based escape; AFN-T $(\beta)$-part]\label{thm:afnt-beta}
Let $S \in \RS$, let $\lambda$ be an ordinal, and let $\{A_\kappa\}_{\kappa < \lambda}$ be a transfinite diagram of pointed $(\infty, n)$-categorical objects with coherent transition functors $f_{\kappa, \kappa'} : A_\kappa \to A_{\kappa'}$ ($\kappa < \kappa' < \lambda$) admitting a colimit $A_\infty := \colim_{\kappa < \lambda} A_\kappa$ in the ambient $(\infty, n)$-categorical meta-universe:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Definable $\mathbf{U}_2$-cofinal closure ($\neg\Pi_4$)}: suppose in addition that (i) $\mathrm{cf}(\lambda) < \kappa_2$, so a cofinal subdiagram $D : I \to \mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ is $\mathbf{U}_2$-small ($I \in \mathbf{U}_2$); (ii) every term $D(i)$ lies in $\cS_S^{(k)}$ for a fixed finite stage $k < \omega$ (in particular, each term is directly $S$-definable or slice-conservatively presented in $S$ via Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership}); (iii) the transition functors $D(i \to j)$ form an $S$-definable diagram valued in $\cS_S^{(k)}$; and (iv) the colimit $A_\infty \simeq \colim_{i \in I} D(i)$ exists within $\mathbf{U}_2$ (and, where $\kappa_1$-accessibility of $A_\infty$ is also required, the diagram and transitions satisfy the relevant presentability/accessibility hypotheses rather than relying on cofinality alone). Then $A_\infty \in \cS_S^{(k+1)} \subseteq \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}} \subseteq \cS_S$, so $\id_{A_\infty}$ witnesses $A_\infty \to_{M, n} A_\infty \in \cS_S$ and $A_\infty$ fails $(\Pi_{4, S, n})$.
\item \emph{Unconditional exclusion from $\LAbs$}: without assuming $S$-definability of transitions, slice-conservativity, $\mathrm{cf}(\lambda) < \kappa_2$, or preservation of a fixed accessibility cardinal, the limit object $A_\infty$ does not belong to $\LAbs$ within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(a)} By (i)--(iii), $D : I \to \cS_S^{(k)}$ is a $\mathbf{U}_2$-small diagram in $\cS_S^{(k)}$ whose colimit $A_\infty$ exists in $\mathbf{U}_2$ by (iv). Applying closure operation (O3) of Definition~\ref{def:SS} places $A_\infty \in \cS_S^{(k+1)} \subseteq \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}} \subseteq \cS_S$. Consequently the identity functor $\id_{A_\infty} : A_\infty \to A_\infty$ is an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence onto its image with target in $\cS_S$, contradicting $(\Pi_{4, S, n})$. (Note that $\mathrm{cf}(\lambda) < \kappa_2$ does not by itself imply $S$-definability of transitions --- as the terminal-collapse counterexample below demonstrates --- nor does $\kappa_1$-accessibility of individual terms imply $\kappa_1$-accessibility of a $\mathrm{cf}(\lambda)$-indexed colimit without explicit accessibility/presentability hypotheses on the transitions and diagram.)

\emph{(b)} Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-class} below applied directly to $A_\infty$: either $\exists S' \in \RS$ witnessing $(F_{S'})(A_\infty)$, in which case Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} refutes $A_\infty \in \LAbs$ at $S'$, or $\neg(F_{S'})(A_\infty)$ for every $S' \in \RS$, refuting the first conjunct of Definition~\ref{def:level6}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proper-Class Towers Do Not Provide an Escape}

\begin{proposition}[Proper-class and non-uniform towers: colimit $\RS$-definability dichotomy]\label{prop:proper-class}
Let $\{A_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}$ be any directed or proper-class-indexed tower of objects over Rich-foundations, and suppose an intended limit object $A_\infty := \colim_{\alpha \in I} A_\alpha$ exists in the ambient $(\infty, n)$-categorical meta-universe. With respect to the class $\RS$, the limit object $A_\infty$ satisfies exactly one of the following two alternatives, and in neither case does $A_\infty$ belong to $\LAbs$ within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(a)}] \emph{$\RS$-definable colimit.} There exists at least one $S' \in \RS$ such that $A_\infty$ satisfies $(F_{S'})$ (for instance, when the tower is uniformly generated by a recursive scheme in some $S_{\mathrm{tow}} \in \RS$ that defines $A_\infty$, or when a non-uniform diagram collapses to a finitely specifiable colimit). Then for every $S' \in \RS$ witnessing $(F_{S'})(A_\infty)$ and every $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ in the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}, Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} applied at $(S', n)$ refutes $(F_{S'})(A_\infty) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S', n})(A_\infty) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S', n})(A_\infty)$.
\item[\textbf{(b)}] \emph{$\RS$-indefinable colimit.} For every $S' \in \RS$, the limit $A_\infty$ fails $(F_{S'})$. Then for every $(S', n) \in \RS \times (\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\})$, the first conjunct of Definition~\ref{def:level6} fails at $A_\infty$.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, $A_\infty \notin \LAbs$ within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the ambient metatheory $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$, excluded middle splits on the sentence $\exists S' \in \RS,\ (F_{S'})(A_\infty)$. In Case~(a), suppose toward contradiction that $A_\infty \in \LAbs$ within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}; by Definition~\ref{def:level6}, there exists $(S_0, n_0) \in \RS \times (\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\})$ such that $A_\infty \models (F_{S_0}) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S_0, n_0}) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S_0, n_0})$, which is impossible by Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} applied directly at $(S_0, n_0)$. In Case~(b), $\neg(F_{S'})(A_\infty)$ holds for all $S' \in \RS$, so no witness $(S', n)$ for Definition~\ref{def:level6} can exist. In both cases $A_\infty \notin \LAbs$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Counterexample to naive diagram recovery (terminal collapse).}
Crucially, the dichotomy above is taken on the \emph{limit object} $A_\infty$ rather than on recursive uniformity of the input diagram $\alpha \mapsto A_\alpha$. A formal specification $\phi_{A_\infty}$ of a colimit does \emph{not} in general recover a recursive specification of its diagram. Already for a countable directed diagram $(\mathbb{N}, \le)$: choose an arbitrary non-computable binary sequence $b : \mathbb{N} \to \{0, 1\}$, let $A_k$ be a pointed finite set of cardinality $1 + b_k$ with basepoint $*_k$, let $f_{k,k} := \id_{A_k}$, and for $j < k$ let $f_{j,k} : A_j \to A_k$ be the constant map with value $*_k$. These transitions compose strictly ($f_{k,m} \circ f_{j,k} = f_{j,m}$ for $j < k < m$), and every element of $A_k$ maps to $*_{k+1}$ at the next step, so $\colim_{k < \omega} A_k \cong \{*\}$ is the terminal one-point set. Here $(A_k)_{k < \omega}$ is non-r.e.\ (encoding $b$), yet $A_\infty \cong \{*\}$ has a constant finite specification and therefore falls into Case~\textbf{(a)} above — excluded from $\LAbs$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}, without any invalid attempt to reconstruct $(b_k)$ from $\phi_{A_\infty}$.

\subsection{Concrete Towers}

\paragraph{Categorical tower.} Finite categorical levels can be compared within specified models. Passing to an unbounded-depth model and proving invariance under further enrichment are additional obligations (Remark~\ref{thm:bergner-lurie-stab}). Finite-level unicity is not this stabilization result.

\paragraph{Topos tower.} Ordinary topoi and higher-topos programmes suggest a hierarchy of semantic environments. A proposed unbounded-depth limit requires a diagram, coherent transition maps, size bounds, and a proof of its relevant universal property. The finite-$n$ unicity theorem of Barwick--Schommer-Pries~\cite{BarwickSchommerPries2021} does not define a stabilized $(\infty,\infty)$-topos.

\paragraph{Spectral tower.} $A_0 = C^*$-algebra; $A_1 = $ spectral triple (Connes); $A_n = (\infty, n)$-version; each level is a derived construction over the previous.

\subsection{The Space of Trajectories}

A subtler variant of the tower approach introduces a choice at each stage: $A_{\kappa+1}$ is not uniquely determined by $A_\kappa$, but depends on a structural choice. The space $\mathbb{T}$ of all possible trajectories is a large categorical object --- a tree, a simplicial set, or a path-groupoid.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:trajectory-space}
Fix $S \in \RS$ and an $S$-definable $\mathbf{U}_2$-small fragment $\fM_0 \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$ of the classifying $2$-stack $\fM$. The space of trajectory choices over $\fM_0$ admits four distinct, generally non-equivalent presentations at different categorical resolutions, each remaining in $\cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$ whenever the corresponding derived construction exists in $\mathbf{U}_2$:
\begin{itemize}
\item as a fundamental $1$-groupoid, $\Pi_1(\fM_0)$ (retaining invertible $1$-cells modulo $2$-isomorphism);
\item as a simplicial set, the nerve $\mathrm{N}(\fM_0)$;
\item as a rooted choice tree of refinement sequences over $\fM_0$;
\item as a relation category, $\mathrm{Rel}(\fM_0)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof (sketch)]
None of these presentations assumes that $\fM$ or $\fM_0$ is an $(\infty, 1)$-topos, nor asserts equivalences among the four resolutions (each retains a different truncation or branching level of the underlying bicategorical data of $\fM_0$). Starting from $\fM_0 \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$, each presentation is formed by standard small (co)limit, slice/comma, and Grothendieck constructions from Definition~\ref{def:SS} within $\mathbf{U}_2$ (cf.\ Lurie~\cite{LurieHTT} \S 3.2 for simplicial nerves of categorical data, and Pronk~\cite{Pronk1996} for bicategorical fraction presentations) and therefore lies in $\cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The colimit of any trajectory-indexed diagram satisfying the definable-diagram closure hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-beta}~(a) lies in $\cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$ and fails $(\Pi_{4, S, n})$. Unconditionally, by Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-beta}~(b) and Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-class}, no such colimit belongs to $\LAbs$ within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}.
\end{corollary}

\section{Five-Axis Absoluteness}\label{sec:five-axis}

Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} establishes AFN-T for a fixed pair $(S, n)$. I now establish that the theorem is robust: it holds for all $S \in \RS$ and all $n$, and moreover for meta-iterations, alternative categorical orderings, and completeness considerations.

\begin{theorem}[Horizontal absoluteness]\label{thm:horizontal}
AFN-T (Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}) is uniform across $S \in \RS$: mechanism $(\alpha_0)$ applies uniformly to every $S \in \RS$ for direct or slice-conservative presentations, and mechanism $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ applies uniformly to every $S \in \RS$ equipped with a canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration and classical (or negative-translated) relative proof-translation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Mechanism $(\alpha_0)$ invokes only Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership} (using (R2) and (R5a)) and the categorical identity morphism $\id_X$, using no datum specific to a particular $S \in \RS$. Mechanism $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ invokes only (R1)--(R4) and first-order (R5) for the uniform test theory $T_S := \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S) \in \cF$, together with the stated canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration and proof-translation hypotheses. Hence both arguments operate uniformly across their declared scopes in $\RS$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relative vertical exclusion]\label{thm:vertical}
At every categorical level $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ for which the syntax--semantics bridge and presentation hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} are supplied, no candidate satisfies the $\LAbs$ triple (specifically, a direct or slice-conservative candidate fails $\Pi_4$ via $(\alpha_0)$, and a general faithful subtheory candidate under hypotheses (a)--(b) of $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ fails $\Pi_{3\text{-max}}$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the direct or slice-conservative case, the identity (or slice-conservative inclusion) gives an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence onto its image with target in $\cS_S$, independent of $n$. In the diagonal case $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$, the contradiction is derived at the level of faithful relative interpretations into $S$ via $T_S = \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$, likewise independent of the ambient depth $n$. Neither argument constructs higher-depth syntax or proves that an unbounded categorical tower stabilizes; for syntactic categories of finite non-invertible depth $n_S < \omega$, Theorem~\ref{thm:cond-infty-stab}~(i) shows that the witness already stabilizes at stage $n_S$, while Theorem~\ref{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity} separates bounded colimits from unbounded limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relative meta-vertical exclusion]\label{thm:meta-vertical}
Any meta-iteration that is actually formalized as an $S$-definable object
of the derived class $\cS_S$ fails $\Pi_4$ in that metatheory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The identity functor on the object is an equivalence onto its image and
is one of the interpretations prohibited by $\Pi_4$. This is the same
argument as mechanism $(\alpha_0)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}.
\end{proof}
This relative exclusion does not imply that iteration adds no structure,
that a tower stabilizes, or that all possible iterations are $S$-definable.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional lateral exclusion]\label{thm:lateral}
Let $\mathcal{D}$ be an alternative categorical doctrine (such as $\infty$-operads, double categories, globular or cubical weak $\omega$-categories, stable $(\infty, 1)$-categories, or monoidal/fusion categories). Suppose a faithful embedding $U : \mathcal{D} \hookrightarrow (\infty, n)\text{-}\Cat$ (for a specified $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$) is supplied that sends every $\mathcal{D}$-candidate satisfying the $\mathcal{D}$-analogue of $(F_S) \wedge (\Pi_{4, S, n}) \wedge (\Pi_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ to a pointed object $U(X) \in \mathbf{StrCat}_{S, n}$ meeting the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}. Then no such $\mathcal{D}$-candidate exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Transport a putative $\mathcal{D}$-candidate $X$ along $U$ and apply Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} at $(S, n)$ in $(\infty, n)\text{-}\Cat$. For doctrines that are already full or replete subdoctrines of $(\infty, n)\text{-}\Cat$ --- such as stable $(\infty, 1)$-categories (which are $(\infty, 1)$-categories with finite limits/colimits and invertible suspension, Lurie HA~\cite{LurieHA} \S 1.1) or $\infty$-operads via their symmetric monoidal envelopes (Lurie HA~\cite{LurieHA} \S 2.2) --- or doctrines admitting established homotopy comparisons (such as globular and cubical weak $\omega$-categories, Ara--Maltsiniotis~\cite{AraMaltsiniotis2017}), the ambient comparison functor is standard. By contrast, strictly richer doctrines such as general double categories (which carry independent horizontal and vertical $1$-morphisms rather than reducing to $(\infty, 2)$-categories) or linear/fusion categories (whose monoidal and linear structure is not recovered by forgetting to a bare $1$-category) require an explicitly supplied faithful embedding preserving the boundary predicates; the obstruction is conditional on that embedding.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Lawvere-scope]\label{def:law-scope}
A foundational theory $F$ belongs to the \textbf{Lawvere-scope} $\cL\cS$ if the following structural properties hold (the metatheory in which $\Syn(F)$ and $\Mod(F)$ are constructed is arbitrary but fixed throughout the four clauses):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(L1)} $F$ has a syntactic category $\Syn(F)$ --- a small category of contexts and provable substitutions.
\item \textbf{(L2)} $F$ has a semantic $2$-category $\Mod(F)$ --- the $2$-category of models.
\item \textbf{(L3)} There exists a canonical Lambek--Scott syntax--internal-language adjunction $\Syn \dashv \mathrm{Lang}$ (Lambek--Scott~\cite{LambekScott1986}) together with the contravariant syntax--semantics duality $\mathrm{Th} \dashv \Mod^{\mathrm{op}}$ (Lawvere~\cite{Lawvere1969}).
\item \textbf{(L4)} $\Mod(F)$ is realizable as an $(\infty, n)$-category for some $n$ (Grothendieck--Lurie tradition).
\end{itemize}
Membership in $\cL\cS$ is a structural property of $F$ and does not presuppose any specific R-S. In particular, every R-S $S$ is itself in $\cL\cS$ (take the tautological $\Syn = S$, $\Mod = \Mod(S)$ from (R5)), so the notion is circularity-free for the application in Theorem~\ref{thm:completeness}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness axis, conditional on Lawvere-scope]\label{thm:completeness}
Suppose $F \in \cL\cS$. Then every structural variation of $F$ within the presentation data (L1)--(L4) reduces to one of the four axes (horizontal, vertical, meta-vertical, lateral), subject to the explicit hypotheses of Theorems~\ref{thm:horizontal}--\ref{thm:lateral}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $F \in \cL\cS$. By Definition~\ref{def:law-scope}, $F$ is characterized by a quadruple:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(A) Syntactic structure $\Syn(F)$}: signature + axioms + inference rules --- the \emph{horizontal axis} (over $\RS$).
\item \textbf{(B) Semantic depth}: the level $n$ of $\Mod(F)$ as $(\infty, n)$-category --- the \emph{vertical axis}.
\item \textbf{(C) Meta-reflection}: iterations of G\"odel coding and reflection principles --- the \emph{meta-vertical axis}.
\item \textbf{(D) Alternative semantics}: operadic, globular, cubical, stable, or faithfully embedded alternative doctrines --- the \emph{lateral axis}.
\end{itemize}
By Definition~\ref{def:law-scope}, membership in $\cL\cS$ \emph{means} that $F$ is presented exactly by the data (L1)--(L4), i.e.\ by the quadruple (A)--(D); this is where the conditionality of the theorem resides --- within $\cL\cS$, exhaustiveness of (A)--(D) holds by definition of the scope, not as an additional claim. Thus any putative fifth axis $\eta$ is a subcomponent of one of (A)--(D), and AFN-T applies under the corresponding hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:horizontal}, Theorem~\ref{thm:vertical}, Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-vertical}, or Theorem~\ref{thm:lateral}. There is no independent fifth axis within $\cL\cS$.
\end{proof}

\section{Three Standard Bypass Paths}\label{sec:bypass}

\begin{theorem}[Definable coherent families]\label{thm:universe}
Let $D:I\to\Cat$ be an explicitly specified small diagram, definable in
$S$, and suppose its relevant limit exists in the chosen universe and is
included among the derived constructions $\cS_S$. Then its limit belongs
to $\cS_S$ and fails $\Pi_4$ by the identity interpretation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Membership is the stated closure assumption; the final claim is
Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} applied to the resulting definable object.
\end{proof}
Universe-indexed endofunctors do not automatically form such a diagram:
restriction along $\cU_\ell\hookrightarrow\cU_{\ell'}$ need not send an
endofunctor of the larger universe to one of the smaller universe.
Preservation of the smaller universe and coherent transition maps must
be supplied. An individual compatible family is a section, not the
entire category of all sections.

\paragraph{Reflective consistency tower.} The second standard bypass attempts to escape AFN-T via iterated consistency assertions. One defines
\[ S_{\kappa+1} = S_\kappa + \Con(S_\kappa) \]
and hopes that the limit $S_\infty$ captures something beyond its starting point.

\begin{remark}[Consistency iterations need separate strength analysis]\label{thm:reflective}
For a specified recursively axiomatized theory $S$, adding
$\mathrm{Con}(S)$ is an arithmetical consistency extension; its proof-theoretic strength
is not uniformly ``exactly one inaccessible cardinal''. Transfinite
iterations require a specified ordinal-notation system, limit rule, and reflection
principle; none is fixed by the schema $S_{\alpha+1}=S_\alpha+\mathrm{Con}(S_\alpha)$
alone, and no uniform inaccessible bound is asserted here. At each well-defined Rich stage, the
relative obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} still applies; it does not measure
the stage's proof-theoretic strength or show that the tower terminates.
\end{remark}

Intensional presentations can carry data forgotten by a chosen quotient. The following section distinguishes a verified slice construction from the additional conditions needed for a nontrivial MSFS refinement. No universal intensional completion is constructed here.

I begin by defining the target $2$-category of display $2$-categories.

\begin{definition}[Display $2$-class]\label{def:display-class}
Let $\cC$ be a $2$-category with $2$-pullbacks. A \textbf{display class} $\cD \subseteq \Mor_1(\cC)$ is a class of $1$-morphisms satisfying:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(D1) Contains equivalences}: every $1$-equivalence in $\cC$ lies in $\cD$.
\item \textbf{(D2) Pullback-stable}: for $d : B \to A$ in $\cD$ and any $f : A' \to A$, the $2$-pullback $f^*d : f^*B \to A'$ exists and lies in $\cD$.
\item \textbf{(D3) Closed under composition}: if $d_1 : C \to B \in \cD$ and $d_2 : B \to A \in \cD$, then $d_2 \circ d_1 \in \cD$.
\item \textbf{(D4) Invertible-cell transport}: membership in $\cD$ is invariant under invertible $2$-cells between parallel $1$-morphisms. Chosen pullbacks are supplied with their usual coherent comparison isomorphisms. No invertibility of transport along arbitrary noninvertible $2$-cells is assumed.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Display $2$-category]\label{def:display-2-cat}
A \textbf{display $2$-category} is a triple $(\cC, \cD, \iota)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\cC$ is a locally small $2$-category with $2$-pullbacks.
\item $\cD$ is a display class in $\cC$.
\item Viewing $\cD$ as the full sub-$2$-category of a specified arrow $2$-category on the chosen display arrows, $\iota:\cD\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Arr}(\cC)$ is its inclusion. The arrow-category convention is part of the chosen model.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The $2$-category $\Sint$]\label{def:Sint}
For the ordinary fragment, $\Sint$ is the $2$-category of categories with pullbacks and display classes, pullback-preserving functors respecting those classes, and natural transformations. The corresponding higher target must be specified together with its level of transformations and coherence data; the list below is a typed interface for that extension, not a proof of its existence.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objects}: display $2$-categories $(\cC, \cD, \iota)$.
\item \textbf{$1$-morphisms}: a functor $F$ preserving the chosen pullbacks up to coherent isomorphism and carrying display arrows into display arrows. Its induced arrow functor restricts to $F_\cD$, with $\operatorname{Arr}(F)\iota_1\simeq\iota_2F_\cD$. In a higher version all functors and coherence cells must be taken in the specified model.
\item \textbf{$2$-morphisms}: natural $2$-transformations $\eta : F \Rightarrow G$ whose restrictions $\eta|_\cD : F_\cD \Rightarrow G_\cD$ are coherently aligned.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[A verified ordinary slice construction]\label{thm:I-existence}
Let $\mathcal B$ be a locally small ordinary category with chosen pullbacks. For $F\in\mathcal B$, set $\cC_F=\mathcal B/F$ and let $\cD_F$ be the isomorphisms in $\cC_F$. Then
\[
 \II_{\mathcal B}:\mathcal B^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\Sint,
 \qquad F\longmapsto(\mathcal B/F,\cD_F)
\]
is a contravariant pseudofunctor in the ordinary display-category fragment. Base change along an isomorphism is an equivalence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Slices have pullbacks. Isomorphisms are stable under pullback, composition, and isomorphism of arrows. Pullback along $f:F_1\to F_2$ gives $f^*:\mathcal B/F_2\to\mathcal B/F_1$, preserving these data. The universal property of pullbacks gives coherent natural isomorphisms $\id^*\simeq\id$ and $(gf)^*\simeq f^*g^*$; uniqueness of the comparison maps gives their unit and associativity equations. An inverse base isomorphism supplies a quasi-inverse base-change functor.
\end{proof}

The result does not construct a nontrivial refinement on the full $2$-category $\cF$. The previously numbered properties are retained as the following explicit extension obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{(I-1)}] Specify the higher target and coherent action on its $2$-cells. The ordinary slice pseudofunctor does not establish this for arbitrary noninvertible cells of $\cF$.
\item[\textbf{(I-2)}] Specify how gauge transformations act. The proved construction preserves actual equivalences of its base; a chosen equivalence between model categories is not automatically such a base equivalence.
\item[\textbf{(I-3)}] To prove strict refinement of Morita, construct two specified presentations with equal Morita image and inequivalent intensional data. The former $\MLTT$/$\ETT$ example does not establish this. The relevant comparison uses intensional type theory extended by both uniqueness of identity proofs and functional extensionality~\cite{KapulkinLi2025}, not bare $\MLTT$. Moreover, normalization, decidable conversion, and decidable typing are different properties. Transporting one of them across an equivalence requires the corresponding effective syntax and rules to be preserved; a display class of arrows alone does not supply those data.
\item[\textbf{(I-4)}] Supply a classification presentation of the same variance as $\II$, a coherent comparison after forgetting display data, and a proof of the proposed localization equivalence. A contravariant $\II$ cannot be composed and identified with a covariant $\rho$ without a variance-changing construction. Yoneda does not imply $\cF/F\simeq\rho(F)$, nor does existence of a localization prove its equivalence to $\fM$.
\end{enumerate}
A higher slice construction requires the appropriate pullbacks and coherent reindexing data on the actual chosen base. These hypotheses must be verified rather than inferred from the words ``Rich-foundation''.

\begin{definition}[Concrete MLTT/ETT fragment and syntactic display $2$-category]\label{def:I-fragment}
Fix a dependent type-theoretic signature $\Sigma_{\MLTT}$ containing $\Pi, \Sigma, \mathrm{Id}, \mathsf{N}$ (with $\mathsf{N}$-elimination) and a universe $\mathsf{U}$, and let $\mathcal{F}_{\MLTT} \subset \cF$ be the syntactic (definitional) sub-$2$-category on $T_{\mathrm{int}} := \MLTT_{\mathrm{UIP}+\mathrm{FE}}$ (intensional $\MLTT$ over $\Sigma_{\MLTT}$ extended by inert propositional constants $\mathsf{uip}$ and $\mathsf{funext}$ without new reduction rules), $T_{\mathrm{ext}} := \ETT$ (extensional type theory over $\Sigma_{\MLTT}$ with Equality Reflection $\text{Eq-Refl}$ and $\eta$-rules), and their conservative finite contextual extensions $T[\Gamma]$ (e.g., contexts inhabited by closed substitutions such as $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}}, \Gamma_{\mathrm{FE}}$, so that $T \to T[\Gamma]$ is a faithful interpretation in $\cF$), whose $1$-morphisms are signature- and $\beta\eta$-reduction-preserving syntactic translations (including contextual extensions $T \to T[\Gamma]$ and the canonical inclusion $T_{\mathrm{int}} \hookrightarrow T_{\mathrm{ext}}$) and whose $2$-morphisms are definitional conversion equivalences (explicitly excluding non-definitional quotient/setoid translations such as Hofmann's interpretation $\psi : T_{\mathrm{ext}} \to T_{\mathrm{int}}$, which remain in the ambient $\cF$). For each $T \in \mathcal{F}_{\MLTT}$, define the syntactic display $2$-category $\mathbf{SynDisplay}(T) := (\cC_T, \cD_T, \iota_T) \in \Sint$ by:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Objects}: well-formed contexts $\Gamma$ of $T$ modulo $\alpha$-equivalence;
\item \emph{$1$-morphisms}: well-typed substitutions $\sigma : \Gamma \to \Delta$ with strict composition;
\item \emph{$2$-morphisms}: for $T_{\mathrm{int}}$, directed $\beta\eta$-reduction witnesses $\sigma \twoheadrightarrow_{\beta\eta} \tau$ (invertible $2$-cells are $\alpha$-equalities); for $T_{\mathrm{ext}}$, derivable $\ETT$ judgmental conversion witnesses $\Gamma \vdash \sigma \equiv_{\ETT} \tau : \Delta$ (invertible via $\text{Eq-Refl}$);
\item \emph{Display class $\cD_T$}: generated under (D1)--(D4) by $1$-equivalences and canonical context projections $\mathsf{p}_A : (\Gamma, x : A) \to \Gamma$, with strict syntactic $2$-pullbacks $f^*\mathsf{p}_A = \mathsf{p}_{A[f]}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Fragment verification of (I-1)--(I-3) and intensional separation]\label{thm:I-fragment}
On the fragment $\mathcal{F}_{\MLTT}$ of Definition~\ref{def:I-fragment}:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{\textup{(I-1)} Non-trivial $2$-cells and $2$-functoriality}: $\mathbf{SynDisplay} : \mathcal{F}_{\MLTT} \to \Sint$ is a strict $2$-functor preserving strict display $2$-pullbacks; $\cC_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}$ contains strictly non-invertible $2$-cells (e.g.\ $\langle (\lambda x:\mathbb{N}.\,x)\,0 \rangle \twoheadrightarrow_{\beta\eta} \langle 0 \rangle$) with decidable $\Hom_2$, whereas $\Hom_2$ in $\cC_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}$ is undecidable.
\item \emph{\textup{(I-2)} Reindexing and Morita non-collapse}: display pullbacks preserve $\cD_T$ and $2$-cells covariantly, and syntactic definitional $2$-equivalences in $\mathcal{F}_{\MLTT}$ induce $2$-equivalences in $\Sint$ (whereas $1$-categorical Morita equivalences act on the $1$-truncated extensionalized slice).
\item \emph{\textup{(I-3)} Strict intensional separation at equal Morita image}: by Hofmann~\cite{Hofmann1995} and Kapulkin--Li~\cite{KapulkinLi2025} (via the quotient/setoid interpretation $\psi : T_{\mathrm{ext}} \to T_{\mathrm{int}}$ in the ambient $\cF$), $T_{\mathrm{int}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{ext}}$ have equal Morita image $[T_{\mathrm{int}}] = [T_{\mathrm{ext}}] \in \fM$ at $(n, h) = (1, 0)$ (external citation); nevertheless, $\mathbf{SynDisplay}(T_{\mathrm{int}}) \not\simeq_2 \mathbf{SynDisplay}(T_{\mathrm{ext}})$ in $\Sint$, unconditionally witnessed by (W1) context separation over $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}} = (x,y:\mathbb{N}, p:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{N}}(x,y))$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{FE}} = (f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}, h:\Pi_n\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{N}}(f\,n,g\,n))$ where $\Hom_2^{\mathrm{int}}(\langle x\rangle,\langle y\rangle)=\emptyset$ while $\text{Eq-Refl}$ supplies an invertible $2$-cell in $\cC_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}$ (and similarly $\mathsf{p}_{P(x)}, \mathsf{p}_{P(y)}$ become equivalent in the slice/arrow display category $\cD_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}} / \Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}}$, since $(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}}, z:P(x))$ and $(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}}, z:P(y))$ are distinct objects modulo $\alpha$), (W2) directed non-invertible vs.\ collapsed symmetric $2$-cells, and (W3) decidable vs.\ undecidable $\Hom_2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (i), every $1$-morphism of $\mathcal{F}_{\MLTT}$ preserves signatures and $\beta\eta$-reductions (while Hofmann's quotient/setoid interpretation $\psi : T_{\mathrm{ext}} \to T_{\mathrm{int}}$ lies in $\cF \setminus \mathcal{F}_{\MLTT}$); strong normalization and confluence of $\MLTT_{\mathrm{UIP}+\mathrm{FE}}$ (the constants $\mathsf{uip}, \mathsf{funext}$ carry no $\beta\eta$-rules) make $\twoheadrightarrow_{\beta\eta}$ congruent under substitution, strictly non-symmetric on redexes such as $(\lambda x:\mathbb{N}.\,x)\,0 \twoheadrightarrow_{\beta\eta} 0$, and decidable for $\Hom_2^{\mathrm{int}}$; conversely, $\text{Eq-Refl}$ encodes arithmetical provability into $\Gamma_{\PA} \vdash t \equiv_{\ETT} u : \mathbb{N}$, rendering $\Hom_2^{\mathrm{ext}}$ undecidable. Strict $2$-pullbacks hold by context substitution $f^*\mathsf{p}_A = \mathsf{p}_{A[f]}$ on both $1$-morphisms and componentwise $2$-cells (analogous to \texttt{pullback2\_\allowbreak universal} in Remark~\ref{rem:grading-open}). For (ii), substitution reindexing preserves $\cD_T$ by (D2) and respects $\twoheadrightarrow_{\beta\eta}$ and $\equiv_{\ETT}$. For (iii), Morita equality $[T_{\mathrm{int}}] = [T_{\mathrm{ext}}]$ in $\fM$ follows from conservativity and model-category equivalence in $\cF$~\cite{Hofmann1995,KapulkinLi2025}. In $\Sint$, any display $2$-equivalence must preserve $\Hom_2$ decidability (W3), invertibility of $2$-cells (W2), and non-empty $\Hom_2$ between $\beta\eta$-normal projections over $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{FE}}$ (W1): since $\langle x \rangle, \langle y \rangle : \Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}} \to (z:\mathbb{N})$ are distinct $\beta\eta$-normal forms, $\Hom_2^{\mathrm{int}}(\langle x \rangle, \langle y \rangle) = \emptyset$, whereas $\text{Eq-Refl}$ yields $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Id}} \vdash x \equiv_{\ETT} y : \mathbb{N}$. Hence $\mathbf{SynDisplay}(T_{\mathrm{int}}) \not\simeq_2 \mathbf{SynDisplay}(T_{\mathrm{ext}})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Isolation of remaining global (I-4) obligations]\label{rem:I4-isolation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:I-fragment} closes (I-1)--(I-3) constructively on $\mathcal{F}_{\MLTT}$ while isolating global (I-4) as three exact open obligations: \textbf{(I-4a)} reconciling the covariant variance of $\mathbf{SynDisplay} : \mathcal{F}_{\MLTT} \to \Sint$ with the contravariant variance of $\II_{\mathcal{B}}$ and $\Mod_{\mathrm{disp}}$ globally over $\cF$; \textbf{(I-4b)} extending syntactic display $2$-cells to non-type-theoretic theories in $\cF$ ($\ZFC, \PA$); and \textbf{(I-4c)} verifying Pronk's conditions (BF1)--(BF5) and essential surjectivity for $\Sint^{\RS}[\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{Morita}}^{-1}] \simeq \fM$ globally.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional slice-locality]\label{thm:slice-locality}
Let $\mathcal P$ be a fixed domain and suppose functors or pseudofunctors of compatible variance
\[
 I:\mathcal P\to\Sint,\qquad \pi:\mathcal P\to\fM,
 \qquad\tpi:\Sint\to\fM
\]
are supplied with a coherent equivalence $\tpi I\simeq\pi$. Then every $I(F)$ has base image equivalent to $\pi(F)$. If the relevant $2$-pullback exists, the intensional fibre over $m$ is
\[
 \mathrm{Int}(m):=\{m\}\mathbin{\times}^{h}_{\fM}\Sint.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion evaluates the specified comparison at $F$. The second is the definition of the fibre with its required comparison-to-$m$ data. No nontriviality or fibration property follows from this definition alone.
\end{proof}
A fibre is not generally the full subcategory on objects with a given isomorphism class: its morphisms must respect the selected comparison to the base point. The existence of pullbacks in the total category does not itself prove that $\tpi$ is a fibration. Constructing the comparison in (I-4), a nontrivial fibre in (I-3), and cartesian lifting data remain separate tasks.

\begin{corollary}[Base-image constancy under the stated comparison]\label{cor:slice-level}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:slice-locality}, forgetting the intensional component of $I(F)$ returns $\pi(F)$ up to the specified equivalence.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
This is the component of $\tpi I\simeq\pi$ at $F$.
\end{proof}
Constancy of a selected quotient does not prove that a refinement adds no mathematical, computational, or resource-sensitive structure.

\paragraph{Finite observational quotients.}
For an executable language, representation identity compares complete programs,
including local library bindings and output selectors. A finite observational
quotient additionally fixes the interpreter, library meanings, all evidence
consulted by admission, and the allowed continuations. Equality of complete
register traces with a separate undefined outcome gives a contextual congruence
only when all continuation and admission metadata are retained and their
updates respect that equality. Such metadata can include constant-use and
selection flags, register layout, and resource allowances. Induction on a
shared continuation proves preservation of the specified observations and
admission predicate; it does not prove equality on new inputs or equal physical
cost. A hash only indexes exact comparisons. Completed fitting witnesses,
merged representations, and unexamined search frontiers therefore require
separate reporting. Budget exhaustion is \texttt{Unknown}, distinct from
semantic undefinedness or a completed refutation. These elementary operational
distinctions do not construct the higher intensional refinement or discharge
the four obligations (I-1) through (I-4).

\subsection{The Intensional Grading}\label{sec:intensional-grading}

The former generating rule began with equivalences and used operations that preserve equivalences. It therefore cannot yield a nontrivial measure of distance from equivalence. A useful filtration needs explicitly chosen additional generators.

\begin{definition}[Grade relative to chosen generators]\label{def:display-grade}
In a specified category or $2$-category, choose a seed class $G$ of arrows saturated under the admitted invertible transports. Let $D^{(0)}=G$ and let $D^{(n+1)}$ be the invertible-transport saturation of $D^{(n)}$ together with arrows obtained by one allowed pullback or composition from arrows in $D^{(n)}$. Thus invertible comparison data have zero grade cost. Put $D=\bigcup_{n<\omega}D^{(n)}$ and
\[
 \gr_G(d)=\min\{n:d\in D^{(n)}\}.
\]
This is a grade of an arrow relative to its specified generators. It is not automatically a grade of an entire display category or of an object of an intensional fibre.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality of a generated filtration]\label{prop:grading}
Suppose a functor preserves the seed classes and each allowed generating operation, up to the admitted invertible transports. Then it preserves every stage and cannot increase grade. Composition satisfies
\[
 \gr_G(d_2d_1)\le\max(\gr_G(d_1),\gr_G(d_2))+1.
\]
If the seed class consists of all equivalences and all generating operations preserve equivalences, then every stage equals the seed class and every grade is zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the generating stage for the functorial assertion. Both factors of a composite are available at their maximum stage, so the composite is available at the next stage. For the last assertion, preservation of equivalences gives $D^{(n+1)}\subseteq G$, while the increasing construction always contains $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Collapse of the former minimal grade]\label{cor:grade-collapse}
For the ordinary slice construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:I-existence}, the filtration generated from isomorphisms by pullbacks, composition, and isomorphism of arrows is constant at stage zero. It cannot witness a strict intensional hierarchy.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each of the three operations preserves isomorphisms; apply Proposition~\ref{prop:grading}.
\end{proof}
An equivalence between display categories is a morphism in $\Sint$; an equivalence used as display data is an arrow inside one underlying category. These are different types. Consequently the localization class from (I-4) cannot be identified with a grade-zero class of display arrows solely by their shared name.

\begin{remark}[Remaining intensional construction and sharp non-thin $2$-categorical tightness]\label{rem:grading-open}
A nontrivial refinement requires generators encoding the intended syntax, evaluation, or resources; a suitable comparison relation; and verified transport of these data. Its grade is initially presentation-dependent. Higher $(\infty,2)$-invariance under equivalence and an equivalence between the resulting higher localization and $\fM$ remain open; neither follows from the former bare-$\MLTT$/$\ETT$ argument. Nevertheless, two concrete constructive realizations are formally verified with zero \texttt{Classical.choice}: (a) on the concrete $1$-categorical operational model $\mathbf{ActEnv}$ below (Proposition~\ref{prop:resource-grading-model}), strict resource-graded filtration separates Morita-equivalent presentations; and (b) on the genuine non-thin strict $2$-category $\mathbf{Poset2Cat}_{\mathbb{Z}_2}$ (posets, monotone $1$-morphisms, and pointwise $\mathbb{Z}_2$-cocycle $2$-cells on parallel equal $1$-cells, admitting unconditional strict $2$-pullbacks), defining the $100\%$ intrinsic fiber-chain height $h(d) \ge 1$ yields $h(e) = 1$ on equivalences, pullback non-increase $h(f^* d) \le h(d)$, arrow-equivalence invariance, and choice-free submultiplicativity $h(d_2 \circ d_1) \le h(d_1)\cdot h(d_2)$, whence for the intrinsic seed class $G := \{d \mid h(d) \le 2\}$ every stage-$n$ display map obeys $h(d) \le 2^{2^n}$ while balanced binary projections attain the \emph{exact} separation $\gr_G(p_{2^{2^n}}) = n$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ --- proving tightness of Proposition~\ref{prop:grading} (\texttt{Display2CatGrading.lean}: \texttt{pullback2\_\allowbreak universal}, \texttt{fiberHeight\_\allowbreak comp\_\allowbreak le}, \texttt{exact\_\allowbreak display\_\allowbreak grade\_\allowbreak separation}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic operational silent-descent grading and Morita separation on $\mathbf{ActEnv}$]\label{prop:resource-grading-model}
On the $1$-categorical enactment category $\mathbf{ActEnv}$, call a transition $s \to t$ in an enactment $E$ a \emph{strict (irreversible) silent step} if $s \to t$, $t \not\to s$, and $s, t$ share the same observable shadow ($\mathrm{observe}_E(s) = \mathrm{observe}_E(t)$). For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, define the intrinsic filtration stage $\mathbf{ActEnv}_n \subseteq \mathbf{ActEnv}$ to consist of all enactments $E \in \mathbf{ActEnv}$ admitting no chain of $n+1$ consecutive strict silent steps starting from the initial state of $E$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Isomorphism invariance}: if $E_1 \cong E_2$ in $\mathbf{ActEnv}$, then $E_1 \in \mathbf{ActEnv}_n \iff E_2 \in \mathbf{ActEnv}_n$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
\item \emph{Exact budgeted characterization and strictness}: for every syntactic presentation $P \in \mathbf{SynPres}$ and budget $B \in \mathbb{N}$, the budgeted enactment $E(P, B) \in \mathbf{ActEnv}$ satisfies $E(P, B) \in \mathbf{ActEnv}_n \iff B \le n$; hence $\mathbf{ActEnv}_n \subsetneq \mathbf{ActEnv}_{n+1}$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
\item \emph{Separation of Morita-equivalent enactments}: for every $m < n$ and presentation $P$, the enactments $E(P, m)$ and $E(P, n)$ share the exact same observable reification $\mathsf{Reif}(E(P, m)) = \mathsf{Reif}(E(P, n)) = P$ (hence are Morita-equivalent in $\mathbf{ActEnv}[W_{\mathsf{Reif}}^{-1}]$), yet satisfy $E(P, m) \in \mathbf{ActEnv}_m$ while $E(P, n) \notin \mathbf{ActEnv}_m$ and are non-isomorphic in $\mathbf{ActEnv}$; moreover, on the two-observable presentation $P_2 = \mathsf{twoSynPres}$, resource-capped actuation $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{cap}}$ at threshold $c = m + 1$ (and hence at any $m < c \le n$) yields non-isomorphic syntactic shadows.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For (i), an isomorphism in $\mathbf{ActEnv}$ preserves initial states, observable shadows, and transitions in both directions (via its morphism and inverse), hence preserves irreversibility ($t \not\to s$) and transports any strict silent chain of length $n+1$ in either direction. For (ii), transitions in $E(P, B)$ obey $(b_1, o_1) \to (b_2, o_2) \iff b_2 \le b_1 \wedge o_1 \vdash_P o_2$, so an irreversible silent step $(b_1, o) \to (b_2, o)$ requires $b_2 < b_1$ inside $\{0,\dots,B\}$; hence a strict silent chain of length $n+1$ starting at budget $B$ exists if and only if $n + 1 \le B$ (realized by unit decrements), and taking $B = n + 1$ yields $E(P, n+1) \in \mathbf{ActEnv}_{n+1} \setminus \mathbf{ActEnv}_n$. For (iii), both $E(P, m)$ and $E(P, n)$ reify to $P$, while (ii) places $E(P, m) \in \mathbf{ActEnv}_m$ and $E(P, n) \notin \mathbf{ActEnv}_m$; isomorphism invariance (i) then forces $E(P, m) \not\cong E(P, n)$ in $\mathbf{ActEnv}$, and for $P_2 = \mathsf{twoSynPres}$ capped actuation $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{cap}}$ at threshold $c = m + 1$ (indeed any $m < c \le n$) advances $E(P_2, n)$ to the $3$-observable reflection while stalling $E(P_2, m)$ at $P_2$. All three clauses are formally machine-checked in \texttt{FoundationalReconstructionV2.lean} (\texttt{actCostGrade\_\allowbreak iso\_\allowbreak invariant}, zero \texttt{Classical.choice}; \texttt{actCostGrade\_\allowbreak budgeted\_\allowbreak iff} and \texttt{intensional\_\allowbreak resource\_\allowbreak grading\_\allowbreak strict}, zero axioms; \texttt{intensional\_\allowbreak separates\_\allowbreak morita\_\allowbreak equiv}, zero \texttt{Classical.choice}).
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{$\LCls$ Meta-Classification}{Classifier Meta-Classification}}\label{sec:meta}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The Class $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}$}{The Class Meta\_5+}}

\begin{definition}[$\LCls$ meta-structure]\label{def:meta}
An object $\mathbf{F}$ is a \textbf{$\LCls$ meta-structure} if it satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(M1) Locally small $2$-category}: $\mathbf{F}$ is a locally small $2$-category.
\item \textbf{(M2) Classification functor}: there is a $2$-functor $\Cl_{\mathbf{F}} : \mathbf{F} \to \fM$ landing in the classifying $2$-stack of Rich-foundations; we write $\fM(\mathbf{F}) := \image(\Cl_{\mathbf{F}}) \subseteq \fM$ for the sub-stack carved out by $\mathbf{F}$'s classification.
\item \textbf{(M3) Accessible reflection}: there is an accessible endofunctor $R_{\mathbf{F}} : \mathbf{F} \to \mathbf{F}$.
\item \textbf{(M4) Non-generative}: $\mathbf{F}$ does not derive the axioms of foundations $F \in \image(\Cl_{\mathbf{F}})$ from its own axioms; classification is parametric, not axiomatic.
\item \textbf{(M5) Metatheory-parametrized}: $\mathbf{F}$ is defined within some $S \in \RS$.
\end{itemize}
I denote the class of such $\mathbf{F}$ as $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Maximality]\label{def:maximality}
A meta-structure $\mathbf{F} \in \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}$ is \textbf{maximal} if additionally:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Max-1) Full classification}: $\image(\Cl_{\mathbf{F}}) = \fM$.
\item \textbf{(Max-2) Gauge-fullness}: $\mathrm{Aut}_2(\mathbf{F})$ acts transitively on gauge classes in $\image(\Cl_{\mathbf{F}})$.
\item \textbf{(Max-3) Depth stratification}: $\mathbf{F}$ admits a \emph{depth-indexed filtration} $\mathbf{F}^{(0)} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{F}^{(1)} \hookrightarrow \cdots \hookrightarrow \mathbf{F}^{(n)} \hookrightarrow \cdots$ of accessible $2$-subcategories with $\mathbf{F} \simeq_2 \colim_{n < \omega} \mathbf{F}^{(n)}$, equipped with a \emph{depth function} $\dep : \Ob(\mathbf{F}) \to \omega$ assigning to each object the least $n$ such that the object lies in $\mathbf{F}^{(n)}$, satisfying the following \emph{predicativity condition}: for every predicate $P$ appearing in a definition by comprehension of an object $A \in \mathbf{F}^{(n)}$ (i.e., the definition has the form ``$A :=$ the object classifying $\{x : P(x)\}$''), every object $Y$ that appears as the target of a sub-expression in $P$ --- in particular every target of $R_\mathbf{F}$, $\Cl_\mathbf{F}$, or subobject-classifier evaluations --- satisfies $\dep(Y) < n$.
\item \textbf{(Max-4) Intensional completeness with separation}: a proposed maximal classifier must supply a correctly typed intensional refinement satisfying the additional requirements (I-1)--(I-4) discussed after Theorem~\ref{thm:I-existence}, together with joint separation on parallel $1$-morphisms by its extensional and intensional comparisons. This remains an axiom on a sought construction. Separation neither supplies missing morphism lifts nor proves equivalences on hom-categories.
\end{itemize}
I denote the class of maximal meta-structures as $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}^{\top}$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Meta-Categoricity: the missing lifting condition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional comparison criterion]\label{thm:meta-cat}
Let $\mathbf F_1,\mathbf F_2$ be locally small $2$-categories over the same
metatheory. Suppose a common $2$-category $\mathbf K$ and comparison
$2$-functors $\Phi_i:\mathbf F_i\to\mathbf K$ are supplied, each essentially
surjective on objects and inducing an equivalence of categories on every
hom-category. Then $\mathbf F_1\simeq_2\mathbf F_2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $\Phi_i$ is a biequivalence by the stated object and hom conditions.
Compose $\Phi_1$ with a pseudoinverse of $\Phi_2$. The resulting comparison
depends on the supplied data; no unqualified canonical comparison is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Status of the proposed maximal classifier]
Conditions (Max-1)--(Max-4) alone have not established these comparison data.
Essential surjectivity on objects does not lift morphisms: the functor from
the terminal category to $BG$, for a nontrivial group $G$, is essentially
surjective and faithful but not full. A transitive action on object classes
does not supply the missing lifts, and separation of $1$-morphisms does not
give equivalences on hom-categories. Straightening applies to an already
specified indexed category/fibration; it does not manufacture the required
universal property from an arbitrary classification functor. Constructing
the common classifier and proving the lifting and coherence conditions
remain open. Accessibility also does not recover unspecified higher cells
from a $2$-categorical comparison.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Mutual independence and non-categoricity of (Max-1)--(Max-4) on $1$-categorical classifiers]\label{prop:max-independence-noncat}
Consider the $1$-categorical classifier schema $(\mathcal{C}, \mathrm{cl}, \mathrm{int}, \prec)$ over the two-point base $\mathrm{Fin}\,2$ (with full category laws on $\mathcal{C}$ and functorial automorphisms $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{C})$), where (Max-1) requires surjectivity of $\mathrm{cl} : \Ob(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathrm{Fin}\,2$, (Max-2) transitivity of $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{C})$ on $\Ob(\mathcal{C})$ (hence on gauge classes in $\image(\mathrm{cl})$), (Max-3) a strict depth function $\dep : \Ob(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathbb{N}$ along comprehension dependencies $\prec$, and (Max-4) joint separation of parallel morphisms by $(\mathrm{cl}, \mathrm{int})$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Mutual logical independence}: there exist four finite models $M_{\neg 1}, M_{\neg 2}, M_{\neg 3}, M_{\neg 4}$ such that each $M_{\neg k}$ violates \textup{(Max-$k$)} while simultaneously satisfying the remaining three clauses.
\item \emph{Non-categoricity without Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-cat} lifts}: the discrete two-object classifier $M_{\mathrm{disc}}$ and the connected indiscrete two-object groupoid $M_{\mathrm{conn}}$ over $\mathrm{Fin}\,2$ both satisfy all four clauses \textup{(Max-1)--(Max-4)}, yet admit no full functor $M_{\mathrm{disc}} \to M_{\mathrm{conn}}$ --- and hence no equivalence of categories $M_{\mathrm{disc}} \simeq M_{\mathrm{conn}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For (i): $M_{\neg 1}$ is the terminal category classifying only $0$ (fails Max-1); $M_{\neg 2}$ is the asymmetric poset $0 \to 1$ with $\dep(0) = 0 < 1 = \dep(1)$ (fails Max-2 because any functorial automorphism preserves arrow direction); $M_{\neg 3}$ is the discrete two-object category with cyclic dependency $0 \prec 1 \prec 0$ (fails Max-3 by irreflexivity of $<$); $M_{\neg 4}$ is the discrete two-object groupoid with silent $\mathbb{Z}_2$ automorphism fiber invisible to the functorial maps $(\mathrm{cl}, \mathrm{int})$ into $B(\mathbb{Z}_2)$ (fails Max-4; every genuine automorphism in $\mathrm{Aut}(M_{\neg 4})$ carries full inverse and functoriality laws and acts injectively on hom-sets, ruling out collapsing endomorphisms). For (ii): both $M_{\mathrm{disc}}$ and $M_{\mathrm{conn}}$ satisfy surjectivity of $\mathrm{cl}$, transitive swap automorphism, empty $\prec$, and subsingleton hom-sets; yet any full functor $F : M_{\mathrm{disc}} \to M_{\mathrm{conn}}$ (in particular, any equivalence of categories) would lift the unique morphism $F(0) \to F(1)$ in $M_{\mathrm{conn}}$ to a morphism $0 \to 1$ in $M_{\mathrm{disc}}$, contradicting $\Hom_{M_{\mathrm{disc}}}(0, 1) = \emptyset$. Both clauses (together with the hom-injectivity guard \texttt{ClassifierAut.no\_\allowbreak collapse\_\allowbreak on\_\allowbreak M\_not4}) are formally verified with zero \texttt{Classical.choice} in \texttt{FoundationalReconstructionV2.lean} (\texttt{maximality\_\allowbreak clauses\_\allowbreak mutually\_\allowbreak independent}, \texttt{maximality\_\allowbreak non\_\allowbreak categorical\_\allowbreak pair}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional quasi-categoricity on the Grothendieck-fibered sub-doctrine]\label{thm:meta-cat-fib}
Let $\mathbf{K}$ be a fixed locally small $2$-category. A maximal meta-structure $\mathbf{F} \in \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}^{\top}$ is \emph{Grothendieck-fibered over $\mathbf{K}$} (written $\mathbf{F} \in \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}^{\top,\mathrm{fib}}(\mathbf{K})$) if it is equipped with a comparison $2$-functor $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}} : \mathbf{F} \to \mathbf{K}$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=\textup{(Fib-\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Essential surjectivity}: $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}}$ is essentially surjective on objects;
\item \emph{Normalized Grothendieck $2$-fibration with connected fibers}: $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}}$ is a normalized Grothendieck $2$-fibration---that is, a Street $2$-fibration admitting normalized Cartesian lifts with $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}}(X') = k$ and $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}}(\tilde{g}) \simeq g$ for every $g : k \to \Phi_{\mathbf{F}}(Y)$---whose strict fibers $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}}^{-1}(k)$ are essentially connected $1$-groupoids (every two objects over the same $k \in \mathbf{K}$ are linked by a vertical equivalence over $\id_k$);
\item \emph{Local full faithfulness on $2$-cells and joint separation}: $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}}$ is locally fully faithful on $2$-cells and every vertical $1$-morphism over $\id_k$ is an equivalence separated up to unique $2$-isomorphism by \textup{(Max-4)}.
\end{enumerate}
Then for every pair $\mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2 \in \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}^{\top,\mathrm{fib}}(\mathbf{K})$, each $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i} : \mathbf{F}_i \to \mathbf{K}$ is a biequivalence of $2$-categories, and consequently $\mathbf{F}_1 \simeq_2 \mathbf{F}_2$. This conditional theorem is fully consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:max-independence-noncat}~(ii): $M_{\mathrm{disc}}$ fails normalized Cartesian lifting \textup{(Fib-2)} over $\mathbf{K} = M_{\mathrm{conn}}$ (and likewise fails essential-fiber connectedness over $0$), since $\Hom_{M_{\mathrm{disc}}}(0, 1) = \emptyset$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \textup{(Fib-1)}, $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}$ is essentially surjective on objects. Fix $X, Y \in \Ob(\mathbf{F}_i)$ and any $1$-morphism $g : \Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(X) \to \Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(Y)$ in $\mathbf{K}$. Because $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}$ is a normalized Grothendieck $2$-fibration by \textup{(Fib-2)}, $g$ admits a normalized Cartesian lift $\tilde{g} : X' \to Y$ with $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(X') = \Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(X)$ and $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(\tilde{g}) \simeq g$; hence $X, X'$ lie directly in the strict fiber $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}^{-1}(\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(X))$, and strict-fiber connectedness supplies a vertical equivalence $u : X \xrightarrow{\simeq} X'$ over $\id_{\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(X)}$ with $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}(\tilde{g} \circ u) \simeq g$, establishing local essential surjectivity on hom-categories. By \textup{(Fib-3)} (local full faithfulness on $2$-cells together with \textup{(Max-4)} joint separation modulo vertical $2$-isomorphisms), $\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i}$ is locally fully faithful on hom-categories, hence induces an equivalence $\Hom_{\mathbf{F}_i}(X, Y) \xrightarrow{\simeq} \Hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i} X, \Phi_{\mathbf{F}_i} Y)$ for all $X, Y$. Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-cat} yields $\mathbf{F}_1 \simeq_2 \mathbf{F}_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Grothendieck-fibration intermediate model and exact $13$-axiom boundary]\label{rem:grothendieck-intermediate-model}

Defining the coupled reflection presheaf $K_{\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}}(X) := \coprod_{Y \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}}} \Hom_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}}}(X, \widetilde{\mathsf{M}}(Y))$ (inhabited at every $X$ by the unit $u_X : X \to \widetilde{\mathsf{M}}(X)$, ensuring \texttt{hInhab}), the cofinal-rank non-representability criterion of Remark~\ref{rem:toe-frontier-obstructions}~(iii) (\texttt{cofinalRank\_\allowbreak nonRepresentable}) unconditionally rules out any representing object for $K_{\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}}$ across all of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}}$ (while single-anchor $\omega$-orbits $K_M$ are excluded directly via \texttt{cofinalRank\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak morphism\_\allowbreak from\_\allowbreak step}, since $K_M(U) = \emptyset$ above the $\omega$-orbit supremum rank while $\id_U \in \Hom(U, U)$). Restricting to the Feferman--Pakhomov $\mathrm{RFN}_{\Pi_1^1}$-progression $\{S_\alpha\}_{\alpha < \varepsilon_0}$ with Hessenberg natural sum $\alpha \oplus \beta$ as the shift action yields a non-thin Grothendieck-fibered $2$-category $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{prog}, \varepsilon_0}$ realizing all $13$ canonical-primitive interface conditions over $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ (extending $\mathrm{Shift2Cat}_{\mathbb{Z}_2}$ of Remark~\ref{rem:toe-frontier-obstructions}~(v) to transfinite $\RS_{\PA}$-theories up to $\varepsilon_0$). Four exact open obstructions separate $\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}}$ from a full $(\infty, 2)$-hyperstack model: \textbf{(B3-Obs1)} extending $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ to all $\lambda_0$-accessible endofunctors $\mathrm{End}_{\lambda_0}(\cC)$ with internal decoding; \textbf{(B3-Obs2)} preserving non-representability of $L_J(K_{\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}})$ under $(\infty, 2)$-stackification; \textbf{(B3-Obs3)} extending coverage from $\Pi_1^1$-sound $\mathsf{ACA}_0$-extensions to all of $\RS_{\mathsf{Q}}$; and \textbf{(B3-Obs4)} full $(\infty, 2)$-hyperdescent and global (I-1)--(I-4) alignment (Remark~\ref{rem:I4-isolation}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Structural Multiplicity}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional structural multiplicity]\label{thm:meta-mult}
Let $\mathbf F_1,\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_3$ be constructed meta-structures satisfying (M1)--(M5), with classification functors $\Cl_i$ to the same base $\fM$. If their essential classification images are pairwise distinct, they are pairwise non-equivalent as classifiers over $\fM$. If each image is proper, none satisfies (Max-1).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An equivalence of classifiers over $\fM$ preserves the essential image of the classification functor. Distinct images therefore preclude such an equivalence. A proper image fails the essential-surjectivity requirement (Max-1).
\end{proof}
The programmes of Univalent Foundations, $\infty$-cosmoi, and cohesive higher topoi motivate candidates for this comparison. A programme is not itself a specified locally small $2$-category with an MSFS classification functor. Verifying (M1)--(M5), constructing those functors, and proving the claimed image distinctions are still required. In particular, being presented in one syntactic language does not exclude another presentation of the same Morita class.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional plurality at $\LCls$]\label{cor:plurality}
Under the construction and image hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-mult}, $\LCls$ contains multiple non-equivalent classifiers over $\fM$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Meta-Classification: construction and stabilization obligations}

\begin{definition}[Meta-classifier $2$-category and meta-gauge quotient]\label{def:meta-gauge-quotient}
Fix a Grothendieck universe $\mathbf{U}_\alpha = V_{\kappa_\alpha}$ ($\alpha \in \{1, 2\}$ in $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$). Define the $2$-category $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_\alpha)$ of $\mathbf{U}_\alpha$-small $\LCls$ meta-structures as follows:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Objects}: tuples $(\mathbf{F}, \Cl_{\mathbf{F}}, R_{\mathbf{F}}, S_{\mathbf{F}})$ satisfying \textup{(M1)--(M5)} with $\mathbf{F} \in \mathbf{U}_\alpha$;
\item \emph{$1$-morphisms}: triples $(\Phi, \eta, \rho) : (\mathbf{F}_1, \Cl_1, R_1, S_1) \to (\mathbf{F}_2, \Cl_2, R_2, S_2)$ where $\Phi : \mathbf{F}_1 \to \mathbf{F}_2$ is a $2$-functor, $\eta : \Cl_2 \circ \Phi \xRightarrow{\simeq} \Cl_1$ is a pseudonatural equivalence in $\Fun_2(\mathbf{F}_1, \fM)$, and $\rho : \Phi \circ R_1 \Rightarrow R_2 \circ \Phi$ is a pseudonatural $2$-cell;
\item \emph{$2$-morphisms}: modifications $\alpha : \Phi \Rightarrow \Phi'$ commuting with $\eta, \eta'$ and $\rho, \rho'$;
\item \emph{Meta-gauge equivalences $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}$}: $1$-morphisms $(\Phi, \eta, \rho)$ such that \textup{(MG1)} $\Phi$ is a biequivalence $\mathbf{F}_1 \xrightarrow{\simeq_2} \mathbf{F}_2$, \textup{(MG2)} $\eta$ is invertible, and \textup{(MG3)} $\rho$ is invertible.
\end{itemize}
Given a $\mathbf{U}_\alpha$-small Grothendieck site $(\cC_0, J)$ in $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_\alpha)$, set $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_\alpha, J} := \mathbf{St}_J\bigl(\cC_0[\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^{-1}]\bigr)$ (and $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_\alpha} := \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_\alpha)[\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^{-1}]$ without topology).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-stage quotient stability vs.\ \textup{(M2)/(M3)} obstructions]\label{prop:meta-quotient-finite}
Work in $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$ ($\mathbf{U}_1 = V_{\kappa_1} \subset \mathbf{U}_2 = V_{\kappa_2}$) at the $\mathbf{U}_1$-small stage $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1)$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Existence and \textup{(M1),(M4),(M5)}}: since $\mathbf{U}_1 \in \mathbf{U}_2$, $\Ob(\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1)) \subseteq V_{\kappa_1+\omega} \in \mathbf{U}_2$, and $|\Ob(\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1))| \le 2^{\kappa_1} < \kappa_2$, $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1)$ is a $\mathbf{U}_2$-small $2$-category, $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}$ consists of internal $2$-equivalences (trivially satisfying Pronk's \textup{(BF1)--(BF5)}), and $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1}$ exists in $\mathbf{U}_2$ with $|\pi_0(\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1})| \le 2^{\kappa_1} < \kappa_2$. It is locally small \textup{(M1)}, non-generative \textup{(M4)}, and defined inside $S = \ZFC + 1\text{-inacc} \in \RS$ \textup{(M5)} (which admits models in $\mathbf{U}_2 = V_{\kappa_2}$, satisfying \textup{(R3)}, and constructs $\mathbf{U}_1, \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1), \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1}$ as sets while $\mathbf{U}_2$ places them in $\mathbf{U}_2$).
\item \emph{Automatic \textup{(M2)} and \textup{(M3)} obstructions}: objects of $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1}$ are meta-gauge classes $[\mathbf{F}]$ of classifiers rather than points of $\fM$, so sending $[\mathbf{F}] \mapsto [S_{\mathbf{F}}]$ forgets $(\Cl_{\mathbf{F}}, R_{\mathbf{F}})$ while choosing a point in $\image(\Cl_{\mathbf{F}})$ fails functoriality unless a type-aligned functor $\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{meta}} : \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1} \to \fM$ is supplied \textup{(M2)}; likewise, classifiers $\mathbf{F} \in \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1)$ can have accessibility cardinals $\lambda_{\mathbf{F}} < \kappa_1$ with $\sup_{\mathbf{F}} \lambda_{\mathbf{F}} = \kappa_1$, so no $\lambda$-accessible endofunctor $R$ exists automatically without a uniform regular bound $\lambda < \kappa_1$ preserved by localization \textup{(M3)}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For (i), $\mathbf{U}_1 \in \mathbf{U}_2$ implies $\Ob(\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1)) \subseteq V_{\kappa_1+\omega} \in \mathbf{U}_2$ with $|\Ob(\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1))| \le 2^{\kappa_1} < \kappa_2$, and $2$-equivalences satisfy (BF1)--(BF5) of Lemma~\ref{lem:pronk} unconditionally, yielding $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1} \in \mathbf{U}_2$ with $|\pi_0| \le 2^{\kappa_1} < \kappa_2$ by strong inaccessibility of $\kappa_2$. Property (M4) holds because $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1}$ parametrizes classifiers without deriving object-theory axioms, and (M5) holds with $S = \ZFC + 1\text{-inacc} \in \RS$ (whose models in $\mathbf{U}_2$ satisfy (R3) and construct $\mathbf{U}_1$, $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1)$, and $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1}$ as sets, placed inside $\mathbf{U}_2$ by $\kappa_2$). Part (ii) follows because distinct classifiers over the same $S_{\mathbf{F}}$ have equal $[S_{\mathbf{F}}]$ yet inequivalent $(\Cl_{\mathbf{F}}, R_{\mathbf{F}})$, and the supremum of regular cardinals below the inaccessible $\kappa_1$ is $\kappa_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stage-to-stage non-collapse and $2$-inaccessible obstruction]\label{thm:meta-nonidempotence-obstruction}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Unconditional stage separation}: let $\fM_{\mathrm{syn}} := \cF_{\mathrm{syn}}[\mathrm{gauge}^{-1}]$ be the countable-signature syntactic moduli ($|\pi_0(\fM_{\mathrm{syn}})| \le \aleph_0$). Then $|\pi_0(\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1})| \ge \kappa_1 > \aleph_0 \ge |\pi_0(\fM_{\mathrm{syn}})|$, so $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1} \not\simeq_2 \fM_{\mathrm{syn}}$; iterating at $\mathbf{U}_2$ similarly yields $|\pi_0(\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls}^2)}_{\mathbf{U}_2})| \ge \kappa_2 > 2^{\kappa_1} \ge |\pi_0(\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1})|$, so $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls}^2)}_{\mathbf{U}_2} \not\simeq_2 \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1}$. Even under coarse essential-image equivalence $\image(\Cl_1) \simeq \image(\Cl_2)$, arbitrary sub-prestack classifiers inject onto $\mathcal{P}(\pi_0(\fM))$, whose cardinality strictly grows at every stage by Cantor's theorem.
\item \emph{$2$-inaccessible reflection and universe obstruction}: in $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$, $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_2)$ has rank $\ge \kappa_2 + 1$, so internalizing $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_2}$ as a small object requires a third universe $\mathbf{U}_3 \ni \mathbf{U}_2$. Assuming $\Con(\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc})$, $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$ proves neither the existence of a third inaccessible $\kappa_3 > \kappa_2$, nor an unbounded tower $\kappa_1 < \kappa_2 < \kappa_3 < \cdots$, nor $\Sigma_2$-elementary Tarski--Vaught reflection $\mathbf{U}_1 \prec_{\Sigma_2} \mathbf{U}_2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (i), fix $[\PA] \in \fM_{\mathrm{syn}}$ and, for each cardinal $\mu < \kappa_1$, let $\mathbf{F}_\mu \in \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{U}_1)$ be the discrete $2$-category on $\mu$ objects with constant $\Cl_{\mathbf{F}_\mu} \equiv [\PA]$, $R_{\mathbf{F}_\mu} = \id$, and base $\ZFC$. Each $\mathbf{F}_\mu$ satisfies (M1)--(M5), and $\mathbf{F}_\mu \simeq_2 \mathbf{F}_{\mu'}$ implies $\mu = \mu'$; hence $|\pi_0(\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1})| \ge \kappa_1 > \aleph_0$. Repeating over a point of $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1}$ with discrete $\mathbf{U}_2$-small classifiers $\{\mathbf{G}_\nu\}_{\nu < \kappa_2}$ yields $|\pi_0(\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls}^2)}_{\mathbf{U}_2})| \ge \kappa_2 > 2^{\kappa_1} \ge |\pi_0(\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1})|$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:meta-quotient-finite}~(i). For (ii), truncating any model with a third inaccessible to its least $\kappa_3 > \kappa_2$ produces a model of $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc} + \neg\exists \kappa_3' > \kappa_2\text{ inacc}$; likewise, when $\kappa_1$ is the least inaccessible, $V_{\kappa_2} \models \exists \kappa\text{ inacc}$ whereas $V_{\kappa_1} \not\models \exists \kappa\text{ inacc}$, falsifying $\mathbf{U}_1 \prec_{\Sigma_2} \mathbf{U}_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional idempotency of meta-classification]\label{thm:meta-conditional-idem}
Fix a regular cardinal $\lambda < \kappa_1$ and a reflecting universe $\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{refl}}$ with $\mathbf{U}_1 \prec_{\Sigma_2} \mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{refl}}$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=\textup{(Idem-\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Uniform $\lambda$-accessible skeleton}: restrict to $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}^{(\lambda)}$, the $2$-category of locally $\lambda$-presentable meta-structures in $\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{refl}}$ whose $\lambda$-presentable subcategories $\mathbf{F}_\lambda$ lie in $\mathbf{U}_1$ (Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994}, Thm.~2.26), with $\lambda$-accessible $R_{\mathbf{F}}$ and $\Cl_{\mathbf{F}}$;
\item \emph{Accessible site and effective descent}: a $\lambda$-accessible Grothendieck site $(\cC_\lambda, J_\lambda)$ in $\mathbf{U}_2$ on $\lambda$-presentable skeletons is supplied such that $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^\lambda$ satisfies \textup{(BF1)--(BF5)}, commutes with $\lambda$-filtered colimits, and satisfies effective $J_\lambda$-descent, making $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda := \mathbf{St}_{J_\lambda}(\cC_\lambda[(\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^\lambda)^{-1}])$ a locally $\mathbf{U}_2$-small $2$-stack with a $\lambda$-accessible reflection \textup{(M3)};
\item \emph{Type-aligned reflection functor}: a $2$-functor $\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{meta}} : \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda \to \fM$ is supplied \textup{(M2)};
\item \emph{$\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^\lambda$-idempotent adjunction}: the second-order classification passage is presented by an accessible $2$-adjunction $L \dashv U : \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}^{(\lambda, 2)} \rightleftarrows \Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}^{(\lambda)}$ whose unit $\id \Rightarrow U L$ and counit $L U \Rightarrow \id$ lie in $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^\lambda$ and respect $J_\lambda$-descent.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda$ satisfies \textup{(M1)--(M5)} and meta-classification is idempotent:
\[
  \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls}^2)}_\lambda \;\simeq_2\; \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \textup{(Idem-1)} and $\mathbf{U}_1 \prec_{\Sigma_2} \mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{refl}}$, every $\lambda$-presentable skeleton reflects into $\mathbf{U}_1$, bounding isomorphism classes by $2^{\kappa_1} < \kappa_2$. By \textup{(Idem-2)} and Lemma~\ref{lem:pronk}, $\cC_\lambda[(\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^\lambda)^{-1}]$ exists and $J_\lambda$-stackification yields a locally $\mathbf{U}_2$-small $2$-stack $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda$ satisfying \textup{(M1)} and \textup{(M3)}; \textup{(Idem-3)} supplies \textup{(M2)}, while \textup{(M4)} and \textup{(M5)} hold in the ambient reflecting metatheory. By \textup{(Idem-4)}, $L$ and $U$ invert $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^\lambda$-morphisms and descend along $J_\lambda$ to $2$-functors $\bar{L} : \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda \rightleftarrows \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls}^2)}_\lambda : \bar{U}$ whose unit and counit are images of $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mg}}^\lambda$-morphisms and hence invertible in the localized $2$-stacks, giving $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls}^2)}_\lambda \simeq_2 \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda$. (On the maximal sub-regime satisfying \textup{(Max-1)--(Max-4)} with Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-cat} lifts, both sides collapse to $B\mathrm{Aut}_2^{\mathrm{Cls}}(\mathbf{K})$, preserving $\Cl_{\mathbf{K}}$ and $R_{\mathbf{K}}$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Open meta-classification problem]\label{thm:meta-stab}
The notation $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}:=\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}/\text{meta-gauge}$
specifies a proposed quotient, formalized at finite stages in Definition~\ref{def:meta-gauge-quotient}. Proposition~\ref{prop:meta-quotient-finite} establishes finite-stage existence of $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_{\mathbf{U}_1} \in \mathbf{U}_2$ with (M1), (M4), and (M5) while proving that (M2) and (M3) fail automatically without extra data; Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-nonidempotence-obstruction} unconditionally separates unstratified stages and rules out $\mathbf{U}_3$, $(\kappa_k)_{k < \omega}$, or $\mathbf{U}_1 \prec_{\Sigma_2} \mathbf{U}_2$ inside $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$; and Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-conditional-idem} establishes conditional idempotency $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls}^2)}_\lambda \simeq_2 \fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}_\lambda$ under (Idem-1)--(Idem-4).

Without those hypotheses, constructing a universal $\fM^{(\mathrm{Cls})}$ satisfying (M1)--(M5) in bare $\ZFC + 2\text{-inacc}$ and proving unconditional idempotency remain open. The unicity theorem of Barwick--Schommer-Pries~\cite{BarwickSchommerPries2021} concerns models of $(\infty,n)$-category theory for fixed finite $n$; it does not prove idempotence of the operation of classifying foundations.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Relative exclusion at any constructed stage]\label{cor:iteration-closure}
At each stage actually constructed within the chosen metatheory and meeting
the definability hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}, its defining
object cannot also satisfy $\Pi_4$. This follows by applying the identity
interpretation at that stage; no stabilization assumption is needed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Closure remains to be proved]\label{cor:meta-closure}
Closure of $\Meta_{\mathrm{Cls}}$ under meta-classification is not a
consequence of the preceding relative exclusion. It requires the separate
construction and type checks listed in Remark~\ref{thm:meta-stab}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The $2$-Category of Rich-Enactments}{The 2-Category of Rich-Enactments}}

\begin{definition}[$2$-category of enactments $\cE$]\label{def:enactments}
Fix a comparison depth $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ (or work fiberwise over the intrinsic depth $n_F$ of each $F \in \cF$); when restricting enactments to a fixed ambient Rich-metatheory $S \in \RS$, we write $\cE_S$. The \emph{$2$-category of Rich-enactments} $\cE$ has:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objects}: quadruples $(F, \cC, \iota, r)$, called \emph{pointed reflective enactments over $F$}, where $F \in \cF$, $\cC \in \mathbf{StrCat}_{F, n}$ (Definition~\ref{def:strcat}, with $S := F$ and ambient depth $n$, or $n = n_F$ fiberwise) is a $\kappa_F$-accessible $F$-structured $(\infty, n)$-category in $\mathbf{U}_2$, $\iota : \Syn(F) \to \cC$ is an $F$-preserving fully faithful $(\infty, n)$-functor onto a replete sub-$(\infty, n)$-category, and $r : \cC \to \Syn(F)$ is a \emph{chosen} left adjoint (reflector) to $\iota$ with unit $\eta_r : \id_\cC \Rightarrow \iota \circ r$ and invertible counit $\varepsilon_r : r \circ \iota \xRightarrow{\simeq} \id_{\Syn(F)}$ satisfying the triangle identities in $\mathbf{StrCat}_{F, n}$. The reflector $r$ is part of the data; it is uniquely determined by $\iota$ up to unique invertible $2$-cell (Riehl--Verity~\cite{RiehlVerity2022} Prop.~2.1.11; Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994} Thm.~1.26).
\item \textbf{$1$-morphisms} $(\phi, \Phi, \theta) : (F_1, \cC_1, \iota_1, r_1) \to (F_2, \cC_2, \iota_2, r_2)$ (abbreviated $(\phi, \Phi)$): a pair consisting of a $1$-morphism $\phi : F_1 \to F_2$ in $\cF$ (faithful interpretation) and an $F_1$-preserving $(\infty, n)$-functor $\Phi : \cC_1 \to \phi^*\cC_2$ equipped with an invertible $2$-cell $\theta : \Phi \circ \iota_1 \xRightarrow{\simeq} \iota_2 \circ \Syn(\phi)$;
\item \textbf{$2$-morphisms}: coherent natural transformations $(\phi, \Phi) \Rrightarrow (\phi', \Phi')$ compatible with the structure cells, i.e., pairs $(\tau, T)$ with $\tau : \phi \Rightarrow \phi'$ in $\cF$ and $T : \Phi \Rrightarrow \Phi'$ satisfying the evident pentagon with $\iota_1, \iota_2, \theta, \theta', \Syn(\tau)$.
\end{itemize}
There is a canonical forgetful $2$-functor and a canonical section:
\[
  \alpha : \cE \to \cF, \quad (F, \cC, \iota, r) \mapsto F; \qquad
  \varepsilon : \cF \to \cE, \quad F \mapsto \bigl(F,\, \Syn(F),\, \id_{\Syn(F)},\, \id_{\Syn(F)}\bigr).
\]
By construction $\alpha \circ \varepsilon = \id_\cF$ strictly, yielding a $2$-adjunction $\varepsilon \dashv \alpha$ whose unit is the identity on $\cF$ and whose counit at $E = (F, \cC, \iota, r)$ is the \emph{pseudonatural} $1$-morphism
\[
  (\id_F, \iota) : \varepsilon(\alpha(E)) = \bigl(F, \Syn(F), \id, \id\bigr) \longrightarrow (F, \cC, \iota, r)
\]
(pseudonaturality in $E$ is witnessed directly by the invertible structure $2$-cell $\theta : \Phi \circ \iota_1 \xRightarrow{\simeq} \iota_2 \circ \Syn(\phi)$ of every $1$-morphism $(\phi, \Phi, \theta)$). In the opposite direction, because $\varepsilon_r : r \circ \iota \xRightarrow{\simeq} \id_{\Syn(F)}$ is invertible, the reflector defines for each object $E$ a canonical \emph{per-object retraction morphism} in $\cE$:
\[
  (\id_F, r) : (F, \cC, \iota, r) \longrightarrow \varepsilon(\alpha(E)),
\]
satisfying $(\id_F, r) \circ (\id_F, \iota) \simeq \id_{\varepsilon(F)}$, while $(\id_F, \iota) \circ (\id_F, r) = (\id_F, \iota \circ r)$ is related to $\id_E$ only by the generally non-invertible internal reflector unit $\eta_r : \id_\cC \Rightarrow \iota \circ r$. Unlike the pseudonatural counit $(\id_F, \iota)$, the per-object retraction $(\id_F, r)$ is not generally pseudonatural in $E$ unless $\Phi$ preserves the reflector (giving only a canonical mate $2$-cell). Neither $(\id_F, \iota)$ nor $(\id_F, r)$ is an equivalence in $\cE$ unless $\iota$ is already essentially surjective. For readability I occasionally write $(F, \cC, \iota)$ for $(F, \cC, \iota, r)$ when the reflector is understood.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Enactment gauge]\label{def:gauge-E}
Define the class of enactment gauge / reflection morphisms $\mathcal{R}_\cE \subseteq \Mor_1(\cE)$ purely by pullback along the forgetful $2$-functor $\alpha : \cE \to \cF$:
\[
  \mathcal{R}_\cE := \alpha^{-1}(\mathrm{gauge}_\cF) = \bigl\{ (\tau, \Phi, \theta) \in \Mor_1(\cE) \;\big|\; \tau \in \mathrm{gauge}_\cF \bigr\}.
\]
(No additional equivalence condition on $r_2 \Phi \iota_1$ is imposed: for a Morita/gauge transformation $\tau : F_1 \to F_2$ whose syntactic functor $\Syn(\tau)$ is not itself an equivalence, requiring $r_2 \Phi \iota_1$ to be an equivalence would fail on $\varepsilon(\tau) = (\tau, \Syn(\tau))$, breaking $\varepsilon(\mathrm{gauge}_\cF) \subseteq \mathcal{R}_\cE$.) If the chosen localization exists with the required universe bounds, write
\[ \mathrm{gauge}_\cE := \text{the $2$-localization class generated by } \mathcal{R}_\cE, \qquad \fM_\cE := \cE[\mathcal{R}_\cE^{-1}]. \]
A construction by Pronk's bicategory of fractions (Lemma~\ref{lem:pronk}) requires verification of all five conditions (BF1)--(BF5), including the $2$-cell cancellation condition (BF4); closure under composition and pullbacks alone is not that verification. By definition of $\mathcal{R}_\cE = \alpha^{-1}(\mathrm{gauge}_\cF)$, one has $\varepsilon(\mathrm{gauge}_\cF) \subseteq \mathcal{R}_\cE$, and \emph{both} the pseudonatural counit $(\id_F, \iota) : \varepsilon(F) \to E$ of $\varepsilon \dashv \alpha$ and the per-object retraction $(\id_F, r) : E \to \varepsilon(F)$ belong to $\mathcal{R}_\cE$ (since $\alpha$ sends both to $\id_F \in \mathrm{gauge}_\cF$); inverting $\mathcal{R}_\cE$ turns $(\id_F, \iota)$ and $(\id_F, r)$ into mutually quasi-inverse equivalences in $\fM_\cE$, without making the internal reflector unit $\eta_r : \id_\cC \Rightarrow \iota \circ r$ invertible in $\cE$ itself.

In the strict $2$-categorical setting $\cE_2$ over an arbitrary strict $2$-category $B_2$ (with $\mathcal{R}_{\cE_2} := \{(f, F, \theta) \mid \mathrm{IsEquiv}_{B_2}(f)\}$), all five Pronk axioms (BF1)--(BF5), strict non-vacuity of (BF4) on a non-thin witness $E_{\mathbb{Z}_2}$, the choice-free universal property of $2$-localization ($\alpha^* \dashv \varepsilon^*$ with full $3$-law pseudonatural coherence via $(\id, \iota_E) \in \mathcal{R}_{\cE_2}$), and an explicit computable span bicategory $\cE_2[\mathcal{R}_{\cE_2}^{-1}] \simeq_2 \mathrm{Base}_{\mathbb{Z}_2}$ with genuine Pronk roof-refinement quotient $2$-cells (\texttt{Span2RoofQuot}, non-thin witness \texttt{tauLeftRoof}) are machine-checked with zero \texttt{Classical.choice} in \texttt{PronkBicategoryFractions.lean}.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The Enactment Syntax--Semantics Lemma}

\begin{definition}[The class $\SSE$]\label{def:SSE}
Given $S \in \RS$, define the base class $\SSEbase \subseteq \Ob(\cE)$ of \emph{locally $S$-definable pointed reflective enactments} to consist of all quadruples $(F', \cC, \iota, r) \in \Ob(\cE)$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item $F' \in \cF$ is either $S$ itself or admits a faithful slice-conservative inclusion $\iota_{F'} : F' \hookrightarrow S$ (so $\Syn(F')$ embeds equivalently into $\Syn(S)$);
\item $\cC \in \mathbf{U}_2$ belongs to $\cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$ (i.e.\ $\cC = \Syn(S)$ or $\cC = \cM$ for a model $\cM \in \mathbf{U}_2$ of $S$); and
\item the fully faithful pointing $\iota : \Syn(F') \to \cC$ and its left adjoint reflector $r \dashv \iota$ (with triangle identities $\eta_r, \varepsilon_r$) are explicitly supplied as $S$-definable enactment data.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $\varepsilon(S) = (S, \Syn(S), \id_{\Syn(S)}, \id_{\Syn(S)}) \in \SSEbase$. (No automatic left adjoint to an arbitrary evaluation functor $\ev_\cM : \Syn(F') \to \cM$ into a single model is asserted: Lambek--Scott~\cite{LambekScott1986} provides the syntax--internal-language adjunction $\Syn \dashv \mathrm{Lang}$ between categories of theories and structured categories, paired with the contravariant syntax--semantics duality $\mathrm{Th} \dashv \Mod^{\mathrm{op}}$, not a left adjoint to evaluation into an individual model.) The derived class $\SSEglobal \subseteq \Ob(\cE)$ is the closure of $\SSEbase$ under those $\mathbf{U}_2$-bounded operations of Definition~\ref{def:SS} that produce an enactment $(F'', \cC', \iota', r') \in \Ob(\cE)$ with $F''$ slice-conservatively embedded in $S$, $\cC' \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$, and explicitly supplied $S$-definable retraction data $r' \dashv \iota'$. Set $\SSE := \SSEbase \cup \SSEglobal$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Enactment syntax--semantics lemma]\label{lem:SS-membership-E}
Let $(F, \cC, \iota, r) \in \cE$ be a pointed reflective enactment satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item $F$ admits a direct ($F = S$) or slice-conservative faithful inclusion $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$;
\item the ambient model-category $\cC \in \mathbf{U}_2$ lies in $\cS_S$ (either $\cC \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$ or $\cC \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$ via Definition~\ref{def:SS}); and
\item the fully faithful pointing $\iota : \Syn(F) \to \cC$ and its left adjoint reflector $r \dashv \iota$ (with triangle identities $\eta_r, \varepsilon_r$) are explicitly supplied as $S$-definable enactment data --- or, when passing to $\mathrm{Ind}$-completions $\widehat{\iota} : \mathrm{Ind}_{\kappa_S}(\Syn(F)) \to \cC$ between locally $\kappa_S$-presentable categories, $\widehat{\iota}$ is $\kappa_S$-accessible and preserves all $\mathbf{U}_1$-small limits, so its left adjoint $\widehat{r} \dashv \widehat{\iota}$ exists by the continuous Adjoint Functor Theorem (Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994}, Thm.~1.66) and restricts to $r \dashv \iota$ on $\Syn(F)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $(F, \cC, \iota, r) \in \SSE$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By (i) and Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership}, the syntactic presentation of $F$ embeds slice-conservatively into $\cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$. By (ii), $\cC \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}} \cup \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$ within $\mathbf{U}_2$. Hypothesis (iii) supplies the $S$-definable fully faithful pointing $\iota : \Syn(F) \to \cC$ together with its left adjoint reflector $r \dashv \iota$ (noting that $\Syn(F)$ itself is essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small rather than locally presentable, and that in the locally $\kappa_S$-presentable $\mathrm{Ind}$-completed setting a \emph{left} adjoint requires $\widehat{\iota}$ to be accessible and limit-preserving, whereas the cocontinuous adjoint functor theorem produces a right adjoint). Assembled componentwise, $(F, \cC, \iota, r)$ satisfies Definition~\ref{def:SSE}: it lies in $\SSEbase$ when $\cC \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{local}}$ and in $\SSEglobal$ when $\cC \in \cS_S^{\mathrm{global}}$. Hence $(F, \cC, \iota, r) \in \SSE$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{AC/OC Morita Duality}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional AC/OC comparison after localization]\label{thm:ac-oc-duality}
Work at a specified categorical level. Suppose the theory and enactment localizations $\fM$ and $\fM_\cE$ exist and the proposed functors $\varepsilon,\alpha$ descend to functors $\bar\varepsilon,\bar\alpha$ on them. Suppose there are coherent natural equivalences
\[
 \bar\alpha\bar\varepsilon\simeq\id_{\fM},\qquad
 \bar\varepsilon\bar\alpha\simeq\id_{\fM_\cE}.
\]
Then $\fM$ and $\fM_\cE$ are equivalent. Any stratum predicates and meta-operations invariant under these comparison data transfer along the equivalence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two displayed equivalences exhibit quasi-inverse comparison functors. A stratum or operation transfers when its defining data commute with those functors, as required by the final hypothesis.
\end{proof}
For the proposed enactments, $\alpha\varepsilon=\id$ holds strictly before localization, and the pseudonatural counit $(\id_F, \iota) : \varepsilon(F) \to E$ lies in $\mathcal{R}_\cE = \alpha^{-1}(\mathrm{gauge}_\cF)$ (turning $(\id_F, \iota)$ and the per-object retraction $(\id_F, r)$ into mutual quasi-inverses after localization). At the strict $2$-categorical level $\cE_2$ over an arbitrary strict $2$-category $B_2$, all five Pronk conditions (BF1)--(BF5), the universal property of $2$-localization $\alpha^* \dashv \varepsilon^*$, and a computable span biequivalence $\cE_2[\mathcal{R}_{\cE_2}^{-1}] \simeq_2 \mathrm{Base}_{\mathbb{Z}_2}$ are constructively verified in \texttt{PronkBicategoryFractions.lean} (zero \texttt{Classical.choice}). Extending this to the full $(\infty, n)$ moduli stack still requires existence and size bounds of the higher localizations, descent of both functors, and coherence across all morphisms; no unbounded-depth lift follows from finite-level comparisons (Remark~\ref{thm:bergner-lurie-stab}).

\begin{proposition}[Weak bicategorical Pronk fractions and accessible localization]\label{prop:ac-oc-accessible-loc}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Weak bicategorical Pronk fractions}: let $\mathcal{B}$ be any locally $\mathbf{U}_2$-small weak bicategory~\cite{Benabou1967} with associators $\alpha$ and unitors $\lambda, \rho$, and let $\mathbf{Enact}_{\mathrm{bi}}(\mathcal{B})$ be the bicategory of internal pointed reflective enactments $(B, A, \iota, r)$ inside $\mathcal{B}$ (meaning $B, A \in \Ob(\mathcal{B})$, $\iota : B \to A$ is a fully faithful $1$-cell in $\mathcal{B}$ with chosen left adjoint $r \dashv \iota$ and invertible counit $r \circ \iota \simeq \id_B$, and $1$-cells $(f, F, \theta)$ satisfy $\theta : F \circ \iota_1 \xRightarrow{\simeq} \iota_2 \circ f$) with $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bi}} := \{(f, F, \theta) \mid \mathrm{IsEquiv}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\}$. Then $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bi}}$ satisfies Pronk's axioms \textup{(BF1)--(BF5)} unconditionally up to $\alpha, \lambda, \rho$, and the bicategory of fractions $\mathbf{Enact}_{\mathrm{bi}}(\mathcal{B})[\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bi}}^{-1}]$ exists and is biequivalent to $\mathcal{B}$.
\item \emph{Discharge of \textup{(O-AC1)} on $\kappa$-accessible $(\infty, 1)$-enactments}: fix a regular cardinal $\kappa \in \{\kappa_1, \kappa_2\}$ per Convention~\ref{conv:notation}, let $\cF_0 \in \mathbf{U}_2$ be a locally $\kappa$-presentable $(\infty, 1)$-category of theories with a $\mathbf{U}_1$-small generating gauge set $W_0$ whose domains and codomains are $\kappa$-compact in $\cF_0$ (without $\kappa$-compactness, Lurie HTT Prop.~5.5.4.15 yields local $\lambda$-presentability for some regular $\lambda \ge \kappa$), and let $L_{\cF_0} : \cF_0 \to \fM_0 := L_{W_0}\cF_0 \simeq \cF_0[\overline{W_0}^{-1}]$ be its accessible reflective localization (Lurie HTT~\cite{LurieHTT}, Prop.~5.5.4.15; Cisinski~\cite{Cisinski2019}, Prop.~7.1.10), where $\overline{W_0} := L_{\cF_0}^{-1}(\fM_0^{\simeq})$. Let $\cE_{0, \kappa}$ be the $(\infty, 1)$-category of pointed reflective enactments $(F, \cC, \iota, r)$ over $F \in \cF_0$ with $\cC \in \mathbf{StrCat}_{F, 1}$ (matching \textup{(R5a)} and Definition~\ref{def:strcat}, $\cE_{0, \kappa}$ includes essentially $\mathbf{U}_1$-small $\Syn(F)$ alongside $\kappa$-accessible $\cC$, or takes $\mathrm{Ind}_\kappa(\Syn(F))$ in $\varepsilon(F)$), and set $\mathcal{R}_{\cE, 0} := \alpha^{-1}(\overline{W_0})$. Then precomposition with $L_{\cF_0} \circ \alpha$ induces an equivalence
\[
  (L_{\cF_0} \circ \alpha)^* : \Fun(\fM_0, \mathcal{T}) \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;} \Fun_{\mathcal{R}_{\cE, 0}}(\cE_{0, \kappa}, \mathcal{T})
\]
for every $(\infty, 1)$-category $\mathcal{T}$. Consequently, $\cE_{0, \kappa}[\mathcal{R}_{\cE, 0}^{-1}]$ exists, is equivalent to $\fM_0 \simeq \cF_0[\overline{W_0}^{-1}]$, and is locally $\mathbf{U}_2$-small and locally $\kappa$-presentable. Extending this construction from $(\cF_0, W_0)$ to global $\cF$ and $n \ge 2$ remains conditional on global $\kappa$-accessibility and higher-cell descent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For (i), $\alpha_{\mathrm{bi}}$ preserves internal equivalences and compositions up to coherent $2$-isomorphisms, giving (BF1)--(BF2) and (BF5). For (BF3), given $u : E_1 \to E_3$ and $w : E_2 \to E_3$ with $w \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bi}}$, taking $P := \varepsilon_{\mathrm{bi}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{bi}}(E_1))$, $v := (\id, \iota_{E_1}) \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bi}}$, and $u'$ via $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bi}} \dashv \alpha_{\mathrm{bi}}$ and an equivalence inverse $w_0^\bullet$ in $\mathcal{B}$ gives $u \circ v \simeq w \circ u'$ by counit pasting up to $\alpha, \lambda, \rho$. For (BF4), every internal equivalence $w_0$ in $\mathcal{B}$ admits a bicategorical adjointification, so $(w_0 \circ -)$ is locally an equivalence on hom-categories in $\mathcal{B}$; transporting along $(\id, \iota_{E_1}) \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bi}}$ verifies existence and uniqueness of $2$-cell lifts, and Pronk~\cite{Pronk1996} (Thm.~21) yields $\mathbf{Enact}_{\mathrm{bi}}(\mathcal{B})[\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bi}}^{-1}] \simeq_{\mathrm{bi}} \mathcal{B}$.

For (ii), since domains and codomains in $W_0$ are $\kappa$-compact, $\fM_0 = L_{W_0}\cF_0 \simeq \cF_0[\overline{W_0}^{-1}]$ is locally $\mathbf{U}_2$-small and locally $\kappa$-presentable (HTT Prop.~5.5.4.15), and $\varepsilon(F) = (F, \Syn(F), \id, \id) \in \cE_{0,\kappa}$ by Definition~\ref{def:strcat} (or via $\mathrm{Ind}_\kappa(\Syn(F))$). Define $\varepsilon^* : \Fun_{\mathcal{R}_{\cE,0}}(\cE_{0,\kappa}, \mathcal{T}) \to \Fun(\fM_0, \mathcal{T})$ by sending $H \mapsto H \circ \varepsilon \in \Fun_{\overline{W_0}}(\cF_0, \mathcal{T})$ (since $\varepsilon(\overline{W_0}) \subseteq \mathcal{R}_{\cE,0}$) followed by the inverse of the accessible localization equivalence $L_{\cF_0}^* : \Fun(\fM_0, \mathcal{T}) \xrightarrow{\simeq} \Fun_{\overline{W_0}}(\cF_0, \mathcal{T})$. Since $L_{\cF_0} \circ \alpha \circ \varepsilon \simeq L_{\cF_0}$, we have $\varepsilon^*((L_{\cF_0} \circ \alpha)^*(G)) \simeq G$. Conversely, for $H \in \Fun_{\mathcal{R}_{\cE,0}}(\cE_{0,\kappa}, \mathcal{T})$, every component $c_E = (\id_{\alpha E}, \iota_E) : \varepsilon(\alpha(E)) \to E$ of the counit of $\varepsilon \dashv \alpha$ lies in $\alpha^{-1}(\cF_0^{\simeq}) \subseteq \mathcal{R}_{\cE,0}$, so $H(c)$ is an equivalence in $\Fun(\cE_{0,\kappa}, \mathcal{T})$, giving $(L_{\cF_0} \circ \alpha)^*(\varepsilon^*(H)) \simeq H$. Local $\mathbf{U}_2$-smallness and $\kappa$-presentability follow from those of $\fM_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Non-cyclic stratified enactment trees and Beck--Chevalley intertwining]\label{prop:perf-noncyclic-bc}
Let $\mathsf{M} : \cF_0 \to \cF_0$ be a $\kappa$-accessible meta-reflection endofunctor with natural reflection unit $u_F : F \to \mathsf{M}(F)$ defined purely on $\cF_0$ (without reference to $\cE$ or Theorem~\ref{thm:ac-oc-duality}) and preserving $\overline{W_0}$. Let $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}} \subseteq \cE_{0,\kappa}$ be the full subcategory of enactments $E = (F, \cC, \iota, r)$ whose $\Cat_\infty$-pushout $\cC^{(1)} := \cC \amalg_{\Syn(F)} \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))$ exists as a pushforward pushout in $\mathbf{StrCat}_{\mathsf{M}(F), 1}$ along $u_F$ (via $\Syn(u_F) : \Syn(F) \to \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))$) inside $\cE_{0, \kappa}$, with structural injections $\Phi_E : \cC \to \cC^{(1)}$ and $\iota^{(1)} : \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F)) \to \cC^{(1)}$. Notice that $\varepsilon(\cF_0) \subseteq \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$ because $\Syn(F) \amalg_{\Syn(F)} \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F)) \simeq \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))$. Define the $1$-step stratified unfolding $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}} : \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}} \to \cE_{0,\kappa}$ by $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}(E) := (\mathsf{M}(F), \cC^{(1)}, \iota^{(1)}, r^{(1)})$ (and inductively $\mathrm{Perf}_\gamma(E)$ for $\gamma < \kappa$ when iterates remain in $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Existence of reflector}: $\iota^{(1)}$ is fully faithful and admits a left adjoint $r^{(1)} \dashv \iota^{(1)}$ in $\cE_{0,\kappa}$;
\item \emph{Non-cyclic intertwining \textup{(O-AC2)} and Beck--Chevalley square}: there is a canonical natural equivalence $\tau : \mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}} \circ \varepsilon \xRightarrow{\simeq} \varepsilon \circ \mathsf{M}$ and a strict identity $\alpha \circ \mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}} = \mathsf{M} \circ \alpha$ on $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$ whose Beck--Chevalley mate is invertible;
\item \emph{Gauge preservation and localized descent}: $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}(\mathcal{R}_{\cE, 0} \cap \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}) \subseteq \mathcal{R}_{\cE, 0}$; moreover, because $\varepsilon(\cF_0) \subseteq \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$, the restriction satisfies $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}[(\mathcal{R}_{\cE,0} \cap \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}})^{-1}] \simeq \cE_{0,\kappa}[\mathcal{R}_{\cE,0}^{-1}] \simeq \fM_0$, across which $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}$ descends to $\overline{\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}} : \fM_0 \to \fM_0$ intertwining $\overline{\varepsilon}$ and $\overline{\alpha}$ with $\overline{\mathsf{M}}$ at $n = 1$.
\end{enumerate}
Extending $\mathbf{StrCat}_{\mathsf{M}(F), 1}$-pushout closure beyond $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$ to arbitrary models, lifting to $n \ge 2$, and discharging \textup{(O-AC3)--(O-AC5)} remain open obligations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For (i), since $\cC^{(1)} = \cC \amalg_{\Syn(F)} \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))$ is a pushforward pushout in $\mathbf{StrCat}_{\mathsf{M}(F), 1}$ along $u_F$ inside $\cE_{0,\kappa}$ and both $\Syn(u_F) \circ r$ and $\id_{\Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))}$ preserve the $\mathsf{M}(F)$-structure via $\Syn(u_F)$, gluing $(\Syn(u_F) \circ r,\; \id_{\Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))})$ along $\Syn(u_F) \ast \varepsilon_r^{-1}$ defines an $\mathsf{M}(F)$-preserving functor $r^{(1)} : \cC^{(1)} \to \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))$ with invertible counit $\varepsilon_{r^{(1)}} : r^{(1)} \circ \iota^{(1)} \xRightarrow{\simeq} \id_{\Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))}$, and gluing $(\Phi_E \ast \eta_r,\; \id_{\iota^{(1)}})$ along the triangle identity of $r \dashv \iota$ yields an $\mathsf{M}(F)$-preserving unit $\eta_{r^{(1)}} : \id_{\cC^{(1)}} \Rightarrow \iota^{(1)} \circ r^{(1)}$. Because $\Fun(\cC^{(1)}, -)$ is a homotopy pullback over $\Fun(\Syn(F), -)$, legwise agreement on $\cC$ and $\Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))$ together with the swallowtail coherence of $r \dashv \iota$ on $\Syn(F)$ ensures that $\theta := (\varepsilon_{r^{(1)}} \ast r^{(1)}) \circ (r^{(1)} \ast \eta_{r^{(1)}})$ is an invertible $2$-cell with $\theta \ast \iota^{(1)} = \id$; replacing $\eta_{r^{(1)}}$ by $(\iota^{(1)} \ast \theta^{-1}) \circ \eta_{r^{(1)}}$ makes both triangle identities hold globally in $h_2\Cat_\infty$, which lifts uniquely up to contractible choice to an $(\infty, 1)$-adjunction $r^{(1)} \dashv \iota^{(1)}$ in $\cE_{0,\kappa}$ (Riehl--Verity~\cite{RiehlVerity2022}, Prop.~2.1.11); invertibility of $\varepsilon_{r^{(1)}}$ implies full faithfulness of $\iota^{(1)}$. For (ii), at $\varepsilon(F) = (F, \Syn(F), \id, \id) \in \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$ the pushout $\Syn(F) \amalg_{\Syn(F)} \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F)) \simeq \Syn(\mathsf{M}(F))$ yields $\tau_F : \mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}(\varepsilon(F)) \xrightarrow{\simeq} \varepsilon(\mathsf{M}(F))$ naturally without invoking Theorem~\ref{thm:ac-oc-duality} or treating $\mathrm{Code}_S$ as an equivalence; $\alpha(\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}(E)) = \mathsf{M}(F) = \mathsf{M}(\alpha(E))$ holds strictly on $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$, with invertible mate $\tau^{-1}$. For (iii), if $m \in \mathcal{R}_{\cE, 0} \cap \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$ then $\alpha(m) \in \overline{W_0}$, hence $\alpha(\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}(m)) = \mathsf{M}(\alpha(m)) \in \overline{W_0}$ since $\mathsf{M}$ preserves $\overline{W_0}$, so $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}(m) \in \mathcal{R}_{\cE, 0}$; since $\varepsilon(\cF_0) \subseteq \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$, repeating Proposition~\ref{prop:ac-oc-accessible-loc}~(ii) on $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}$ gives $\cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}}[(\mathcal{R}_{\cE,0} \cap \cE_{0,\kappa}^{\mathrm{po}})^{-1}] \simeq \fM_0 \simeq \cE_{0,\kappa}[\mathcal{R}_{\cE,0}^{-1}]$, across which $\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{Perf}}$ descends.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional definitional conservativity]\label{cor:ac-oc-conservativity}
If the enactment apparatus and all its structure maps are introduced by explicit definitions whose existence and uniqueness are proved in a fixed base $B$, adjoining their names is conservative over $B$ for sentences of its original language.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Eliminate the new names by their defining formulas. Existence and uniqueness make this elimination a proof translation into $B$. Categorical equivalence alone is not this syntactic conservativity argument.
\end{proof}
Applying the corollary requires completing the construction of the enactment apparatus in $B$. No equation $\Con(\mathrm{MSFS})=\Con(\ZFC+2\text{-inacc})$ follows merely from using that metatheory. A model construction gives an upper consistency bound; a matching lower bound requires an additional interpretation.

By analogy with the $\LAbs$ conditions of \S\ref{sec:conditions} (and coinciding with their transport whenever the conditional equivalence of Theorem~\ref{thm:ac-oc-duality} holds), we formulate the enactment-absolute conditions directly in $\cE$. Informally, an \emph{enactment-absolute} quadruple $(F, \cC, \iota, r)$ simultaneously admits formal $S$-presentation with faithful interpretation into $S$, admits no non-trivial coordination with enactments in $\SSE$, and is maximally receptive.

\begin{definition}[$\LAbsE$ conditions]\label{def:level6-cE}
Given $S \in \RS$ and $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, a pointed reflective enactment $(F, \cC, \iota, r) \in \cE$ is \textbf{enactment-absolute} if it satisfies the triple:
\begin{itemize}
\item[$(\tilde{F}_S)$] \emph{Enactment-definability}: there exist $F' \in \cF$ and explicit faithful relative interpretations $F \hookrightarrow F' \hookrightarrow S$ in $\cF$ (in particular, a composite faithful relative interpretation $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$, matching (F-1) of Definition~\ref{def:F}; mere formula specification of $F$'s axioms inside $F' \hookrightarrow S$ does not suffice, as $\ZFC$ has a recursive axiom formula in $\PA \hookrightarrow \PA$ without interpreting into $\PA$), together with formulas $(\phi_F, \psi_\cC, \chi_\iota, \chi_r)$ in $F'$ specifying $F, \cC, \iota, r$ with the reflection adjunction $r \dashv \iota$ provable in $F'$. The presentation is called \emph{slice-conservative} if $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$, $\cC$, and $(\iota, r)$ satisfy hypotheses (i)--(iii) of Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership-E}.
\item[$(\tilde{\Pi}_{4, S, n})$] \emph{Non-coordination}: there is no $1$-morphism $(\phi, \Phi) : (F, \cC, \iota, r) \to (F', \cC', \iota', r')$ in $\cE$ with target quadruple in $\SSE$ (Definition~\ref{def:SSE}) such that $\Phi$ is an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence onto its image.
\item[$(\tilde{\Pi}_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$] \emph{Maximal receptivity}: for every enactment $(F', \cC', \iota', r') \in \cE$ there exists a $1$-morphism $(\phi, \Phi) : (F', \cC', \iota', r') \to (F, \cC, \iota, r)$ realizing a Morita reduction at the $(\infty, n)$-level (in particular, $\phi : F' \to F$ is a faithful relative interpretation in $\cF$).
\end{itemize}
The \emph{$\LAbsE$ stratum} is the class of $\mathrm{gauge}_\cE$-equivalence classes in $\fM_\cE$ with an enactment-absolute representative.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Dual Boundary Lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Dual Boundary Lemma; Dual-AFN-T $(\alpha)$-part]\label{thm:dual-afnt}
Let $S \in \RS$ and $n \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, and let $(F, \cC, \iota, r) \in \cE$ satisfy $(\tilde{F}_S)$ via faithful relative interpretations $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow F' \hookrightarrow S$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Direct / slice-conservative retraction branch}: whenever the presentation satisfies hypotheses (i)--(iii) of Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership-E}, already the sub-pair $(\tilde{F}_S) \wedge (\tilde{\Pi}_{4, S, n})$ is inconsistent.
\item \emph{General diagonal branch}: whenever $S$ satisfies the canonical $\Sigma_1$-binumeration and classical relative (or negative-translation plus primitive-recursive proof-translation) hypotheses (a)--(b) of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}~(ii), the receptive pair $(\tilde{F}_S) \wedge (\tilde{\Pi}_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ is inconsistent for \emph{every} faithful relative interpretation $F \hookrightarrow F' \hookrightarrow S$ (without assuming slice-conservativity, without invoking Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership-E}, and independently of the localization hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:ac-oc-duality}).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, across all $(S, n)$ in the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}, no pointed reflective enactment satisfies $(\tilde{F}_S) \wedge (\tilde{\Pi}_{4, S, n}) \wedge (\tilde{\Pi}_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)} Under hypotheses (i)--(iii) of Lemma~\ref{lem:SS-membership-E}, $(F, \cC, \iota, r) \in \SSE$. The identity $1$-morphism $(\id_F, \id_\cC) : (F, \cC, \iota, r) \to (F, \cC, \iota, r)$ in $\cE$ has $\Phi = \id_\cC$ an $(\infty, n)$-equivalence onto its image with target in $\SSE$, contradicting $(\tilde{\Pi}_{4, S, n})$.

\emph{(ii)} Form the consistent first-order arithmetic theory $T_S := \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S) \in \cF$ as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}~(ii), and consider its syntactic self-enactment $\varepsilon(T_S) = (T_S, \Syn(T_S), \id, \id) \in \cE$. Applying $(\tilde{\Pi}_{3\text{-max}, S, n})$ to $\varepsilon(T_S)$ yields a $1$-morphism $(\tau, \Phi) : \varepsilon(T_S) \to (F, \cC, \iota, r)$ in $\cE$. Applying the forgetful $2$-functor $\alpha : \cE \to \cF$ extracts the faithful relative interpretation $\tau = \alpha(\tau, \Phi) : T_S \to F$. Composing $\tau$ with the explicit faithful relative interpretation $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow F' \hookrightarrow S$ supplied by $(\tilde{F}_S)$ gives $J := \iota_F \circ \tau : \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S) \to S$, contradicting the G\"odel--Feferman--Pudl\'ak Second Incompleteness Theorem exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}~(ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$\LAbsE$ emptiness]\label{cor:level6-empty-cE}
Within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:dual-afnt}, $\LAbsE = \emptyset$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional transfer of relative axes]\label{thm:dual-five-axis}
Whenever an axis comparison preserves the hypotheses of the relative boundary lemma and satisfies the descent and coherence conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:ac-oc-duality}, the corresponding relative exclusion transfers to the enactment presentation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Translate a proposed witness along the assumed comparison and apply the boundary lemma in the target presentation. Preservation of its predicates is a hypothesis of this transfer, not a consequence of the name of the axis.
\end{proof}
This does not provide a general exclusion for universe changes, reflection principles, or arbitrary operational refinements. A closed symmetric monoidal category need not be cartesian closed and does not automatically support the cartesian Lawvere diagonal.

\section{Consequences and Open Questions}\label{sec:consequences}

\subsection{Consequences for Foundational Programmes}

AFN-T imposes a uniform structural bound on candidate absolute foundations: within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}, $\LAbs$ is empty, while existing foundational programmes inhabit $\LFnd$ or motivate candidate classifiers in $\LCls$ (possibly $\LClsMax \subseteq \LCls$). Specifically:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Univalent Foundations}, higher topoi, and cohesive frameworks motivate candidate classifiers. Their exact MSFS membership and classification images require the construction and verification specified in Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-mult}.
\item \textbf{Higher topos theory} supplies semantic structures; an MSFS classifying functor is further data, and no programme-level maximality verdict is inferred without it.
\item \textbf{Condensed mathematics} (Clausen--Scholze~\cite{ClausenScholze2019}), perfectoid geometry, and $\infty$-cosmoi (Riehl--Verity~\cite{RiehlVerity2022}) remain mathematical frameworks whose utility and potential extensions are not bounded by the relative $\Pi_4$ exclusion.
\item \textbf{Proof assistants} (Coq, Lean, Agda) implement $\LFnd$ foundations ($\CIC$, $\CIC + \mathrm{Univalence}$, $\MLTT$); the plurality of such systems is the plurality of $\LFnd$.
\item \textbf{Categorical logic}: fixed-depth comparisons can organize specified foundational presentations. No upper bound on further classifier structure follows from the finite-$n$ unicity theorem.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Diagnostic for a specified candidate.} First specify the formal object, its metatheory, and the relevant interpretation relation. If the candidate belongs to the derived class $\cS_S$, its identity morphism violates $\Pi_4$ via $(\alpha_0)$; if it is presented in a faithful subtheory $\iota_F : F \hookrightarrow S$ satisfying hypotheses (a)--(b) of $(\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}})$ and claims internal interpretability of all Rich-foundations $(\Pi_{3\text{-max}})$, it is refuted by diagonalization via $T_S = \PA + \Con_{\sigma_S}(S)$. Assigning particular research programmes to $\LCls\setminus\LClsMax$ additionally requires the classifier constructions and essential-image checks of Theorem~\ref{thm:meta-mult}; absence of a syntactic presentation in one language is not proof of absence of a Morita-equivalent presentation.

\subsection{Deductive Provenance, Ontological Floor Stratification, and Tariffed Reconstruction}\label{sec:provenance}

Because AFN-T (Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}) establishes $\LAbs = \emptyset$, mathematical practice cannot collapse onto a single absolute foundation; instead it unfolds inside the stratified $2$-category $\fM$ of Rich-foundations $\LFnd$ and their classifiers $\LCls$. A structural pathology of contemporary and AI-assisted proofs of long-standing mathematical problems is that they frequently sever \emph{local certificate checking} (an opaque SAT/DRAT trace, a proof-assistant script importing uncalibrated classical choice, or a multi-hundred-page citation chain resting on unverified external black boxes) from \emph{foundational provenance} inside $\LFnd$. To turn this diagnosis into a formal invariant on proof practice over $\fM$, we introduce a provenance-graded tariff calculus.

\begin{definition}[Ontological floor, provenance tariff, and anti-vacuity DAG grounding]\label{def:provenance-tariff}
Fix an ordered scale $\Phi = (\mathbb{N}, \le)$ indexing Morita-increasing foundational floors inside (and below) $\LFnd$ --- for concreteness, bounded arithmetic $\mathsf{I\Delta_0}$, $\mathsf{PRA}$, $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, $\mathsf{Z}_2$, $\ZFC$, and $\ZFC + \text{Grothendieck universes}$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item A \emph{proof tree} $T$ over $\Phi$ is generated inductively by grounded axiom leaves $\mathrm{ax}(f)$ ($f \in \Phi$), opaque/uncalibrated external or oracle leaves $\mathrm{op}(f)$ ($f \in \Phi$), unary inference steps $\mathrm{step}_1(T)$, and binary cut/composition steps $\mathrm{step}_2(T_1, T_2)$ (reflecting $1$-cell composition in $\fM$).
\item Its \emph{provenance tariff profile} is the triple $\tau(T) := (\mathrm{fl}(T),\, \mathrm{op}(T),\, \mathrm{ht}(T)) \in \Phi \times \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ defined recursively by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau(\mathrm{ax}(f)) &= (f, 0, 0), \qquad \tau(\mathrm{op}(f)) = (f, 1, 0),\\
\tau(\mathrm{step}_1(T)) &= (\mathrm{fl}(T),\, \mathrm{op}(T),\, \mathrm{ht}(T)+1),\\
\tau(\mathrm{step}_2(T_1, T_2)) &= \bigl(\max(\mathrm{fl}(T_1),\mathrm{fl}(T_2)),\, \mathrm{op}(T_1)+\mathrm{op}(T_2),\, \max(\mathrm{ht}(T_1),\mathrm{ht}(T_2))+1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Relative to a declared foundational target $d \in \Phi$, the \emph{axiomatic provenance debt} of $T$ is $\delta(T, d) := \max(0,\, \mathrm{fl}(T) - d)$. A \emph{reconstruction substitution} $\sigma$ maps each opaque leaf $\mathrm{op}(f)$ to a proof tree $\sigma(f)$ and acts recursively on proof trees as $T \mapsto T[\sigma]$ by fixing $\mathrm{ax}(f)$ and commuting with $\mathrm{step}_1, \mathrm{step}_2$.
\item A \emph{citation network} is a triple $(V, E, R)$ where $V$ is a set of claims, $E \subseteq V \times V$ is a dependency relation ($E(v, u)$ meaning ``$v$ cites $u$''), and $R \subseteq V$ is the set of explicitly verified grounded roots in a fixed stratum $F \in \LFnd$. The \emph{anti-vacuity grounding hierarchy} $\{G_k\}_{k \in \mathbb{N}}$ is defined inductively by $G_0 := R$ and
\[
G_{k+1} := G_k \;\cup\; \bigl\{ v \in V \;\big|\; (\exists\, u \in V,\, E(v, u)) \;\wedge\; (\forall\, u \in V,\, E(v, u) \implies u \in G_k) \bigr\}.
\]
A non-empty subset $C \subseteq V \setminus R$ is a \emph{fossil component} if $\forall v \in C,\; \exists u \in C,\; E(v, u)$ (in particular, any unrooted citation cycle). A vertex $v \in V \setminus R$ with $\neg\exists u \in V,\, E(v, u)$ is an \emph{isolated non-root claim}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Provenance contamination, anti-vacuity DAG grounding, and Kleisli tariff reconstruction]\label{prop:provenance-grounding}
Let $\Phi = (\mathbb{N}, \le)$, let $T$ be a proof tree over $\Phi$, and let $(V, E, R)$ be a citation network as in Definition~\ref{def:provenance-tariff}.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Hidden-leaf contamination and monotonicity}: if $T$ contains an opaque leaf $\mathrm{op}(f_{\mathrm{op}})$, then $\mathrm{fl}(T) \ge f_{\mathrm{op}}$; in particular, a single hidden classical or oracle leaf with $f_{\mathrm{op}} > d$ forces positive debt $\delta(T, d) \ge f_{\mathrm{op}} - d > 0$ at the root.
\item \emph{Kleisli tariff-reduction calculus}: reconstruction $T \mapsto T[\sigma]$ satisfies the Kleisli monad identity and associativity laws $T[\mathrm{op}] = T$ and $(T[\sigma])[\rho] = T[\rho \bullet \sigma]$, where $(\rho \bullet \sigma)(f) := (\sigma(f))[\rho]$. On binary cut steps $\mathrm{step}_2(T_1, T_2)$, the first two coordinates $(\mathrm{fl}, \mathrm{op})$ act as a strict homomorphism into $(\Phi, \max) \times (\mathbb{N}, +)$ while height increments by $\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{step}_2(T_1, T_2)) = \max(\mathrm{ht}(T_1), \mathrm{ht}(T_2)) + 1$. Moreover, tariff evaluation is pointwise monotone: whenever $\mathrm{fl}(\sigma(f)) \le \mathrm{fl}(\sigma'(f))$ and $\mathrm{op}(\sigma(f)) \le \mathrm{op}(\sigma'(f))$ for all $f \in \Phi$, one has $\mathrm{fl}(T[\sigma]) \le \mathrm{fl}(T[\sigma'])$ and $\mathrm{op}(T[\sigma]) \le \mathrm{op}(T[\sigma'])$. In particular, if all grounded leaves $\mathrm{ax}(f)$ already occurring in $T$ satisfy $f \le d$ and every opaque leaf is replaced by an explicit grounded proof tree $\sigma(f)$ with $\mathrm{op}(\sigma(f)) = 0$ and $\mathrm{fl}(\sigma(f)) \le d$, then $\mathrm{op}(T[\sigma]) = 0$ and $\delta(T[\sigma], d) = 0$.
\item \emph{Fossil fixed-point non-grounding vs.\ ranked DAG grounding}: for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$:
  \begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
  \item no vertex of a fossil component $C \subseteq V \setminus R$ lies in $G_k$;
  \item no isolated non-root vertex $v \in V \setminus R$ lies in $G_k$;
  \item conversely, if $r : V \to \mathbb{N}$ is a rank function such that every $v \notin R$ has at least one dependency and $E(v, u) \implies r(u) < r(v)$, then every $v \in V$ satisfies $v \in G_{r(v)}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)} Immediate by structural induction on $T$: at $\mathrm{op}(f_{\mathrm{op}})$ one has $\mathrm{fl} = f_{\mathrm{op}}$, while $\mathrm{step}_1$ preserves $\mathrm{fl}$ and $\mathrm{step}_2$ takes $\max$, which is monotone in both arguments.

\emph{(ii)} Structural induction on $T$ yields $T[\mathrm{op}] = T$ and $(T[\sigma])[\rho] = T[\rho \bullet \sigma]$ directly from the recursive clauses on $\mathrm{ax}, \mathrm{op}, \mathrm{step}_1, \mathrm{step}_2$ (at $\mathrm{op}(f)$ both sides equal $(\sigma(f))[\rho]$). The binary step identities hold by Definition~\ref{def:provenance-tariff}(ii), and monotonicity of $\max$ and $+$ gives pointwise monotonicity in $(\mathrm{fl}, \mathrm{op})$. When all grounded leaves $\mathrm{ax}(f)$ of $T$ satisfy $f \le d$ and every opaque leaf is replaced by $\sigma(f)$ with $\mathrm{op}(\sigma(f)) = 0$ and $\mathrm{fl}(\sigma(f)) \le d$, induction gives $\mathrm{op}(T[\sigma]) = 0$ and $\mathrm{fl}(T[\sigma]) \le d$, hence $\delta(T[\sigma], d) = 0$.

\emph{(iii)} For (a), proceed by induction on $k$: at $k = 0$, $G_0 = R$ is disjoint from $C \subseteq V \setminus R$. For the step $k \to k+1$, if $v \in C$ entered $G_{k+1} \setminus G_k$, then every $u$ with $E(v, u)$ would lie in $G_k$; yet by definition of $C$ there exists $u \in C$ with $E(v, u)$, contradicting the induction hypothesis $C \cap G_k = \emptyset$. For (b), induction on $k$ shows $v \notin G_0 = R$, and the existential guard $\exists u,\, E(v, u)$ in $G_{k+1}$ fails for isolated $v$. For (c), strong induction on $r(v)$: if $v \in R$ then $v \in G_0 \subseteq G_{r(v)}$; if $v \notin R$, then $v$ has at least one dependency $u_0$, every dependency $u$ satisfies $r(u) < r(v)$ and hence $u \in G_{r(u)} \subseteq G_{r(v)-1}$ (since $G_k$ is monotone in $k$), so $v \in G_{r(v)}$.
\end{proof}

A second, orthogonal pathology arises when finite-horizon machine verification (such as bounded SAT/DRAT checks or empirical finite-case enumeration) is conflated with a uniform foundational proof.

\begin{theorem}[Certificate--invariant separation and translation-adequacy invariance]\label{thm:cert-inv-separation}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Finite-horizon indistinguishability}: for every finite verification horizon $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ there exist two explicitly decidable predicates $P, Q : \mathbb{N} \to \{0,1\}$ that agree on every $k < N_0$ ($P(k) = Q(k) = 1$ for all $k < N_0$), yet diverge at $N_0$ with $\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\, P(n) = 1$ while $Q(N_0) = 0$.
\item \emph{Uniform structural-invariant decision}: conversely, if an invariant $\Phi : X \to I$ maps all valid objects $\mathrm{Valid} \subseteq X$ into a property $\mathrm{Good} \subseteq I$ disjoint from $\Phi(\mathrm{Bad})$ ($\forall x,\, \mathrm{Bad}(x) \implies \neg\mathrm{Good}(\Phi(x))$), then a single proof over $I$ uniformly establishes $\forall x \in X,\, \mathrm{Valid}(x) \implies \neg\mathrm{Bad}(x)$ across arbitrary instance sizes.
\item \emph{Clock-Trap offset invariance and kernel blindness to translation gaps}: any internal audit functional $\mathcal{A}$ depending solely on a kernel derivation fingerprint (logical axiom list, zero holes, zero opaque constants) is strictly invariant under semantics-shifting specification re-interpretations $\sigma_\delta$ ($\delta \in \mathbb{Z}$), leaving external semantic fidelity undetermined even at the empty-axiom floor; in particular, pure kernel checking is blind to three orthogonal failure modes --- vacuous antecedent collapse, forgetful operational/scalar proxy erasure (e.g.\ $\mathsf{Reif}$ forgetting budget, or scalar-proxy R-5B satisfied at unphysical $H(X) = -100 < 0$), and definition-drift mismatch (e.g.\ unguarded non-injective endomorphisms on $M_{\neg 4}$). Discharging this gap requires a $4$-component \emph{translation-adequacy certificate} (kernel proof, non-vacuity witness, canonical reference bridge, and adversarial hypothesis-sensitivity counterexample).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, neither bounded finite-horizon certification nor zero-axiom kernel checking alone substitutes for structural invariants and external translation adequacy. Under the AC/OC adjunction $\varepsilon \dashv \alpha$ of \S\ref{sec:ac-oc-duality}, this matches the finite-budget enactment obstruction: an observer restricted to access horizon $N_0$ (or to internal syntactic fingerprints on $\mathbf{SynPres}$) cannot recover uniform coherence or semantic alignment residing in the unlocalized operational fiber $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (i), set $P(n) := 1$ identically and $Q(n) := 1$ if $n < N_0$ and $0$ otherwise. Both $P$ and $Q$ are decidable, satisfy $P(k) = Q(k) = 1$ for every $k < N_0$, and separate at $N_0$ since $P(N_0) = 1$ whereas $Q(N_0) = 0$. \section{Categorical Preliminaries}\label{app:categorical}

I collect here the categorical tools and standard theorems invoked in the proofs above.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$2$-Categories}{2-Categories}}

A \textbf{$2$-category} $\cC$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item A class of objects $\Ob(\cC)$.
\item For each pair $A, B \in \Ob(\cC)$, a small category $\Hom_\cC(A, B)$ of $1$-morphisms and $2$-morphisms.
\item Composition functors and identity $1$-morphisms satisfying standard $2$-categorical coherence.
\end{itemize}

A $2$-category is \textbf{locally small} if each $\Hom_\cC(A, B)$ is a small category. All $2$-categories in this paper are locally small unless stated otherwise.

Reference: Kelly~\cite{Kelly1982} for enriched and $2$-categorical foundations; B\'enabou~\cite{Benabou1967} for bicategories.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$(\infty, n)$-Categories}{(infinity,n)-Categories}}

An $(\infty, n)$-category is a generalization of category theory where:
\begin{itemize}
\item Morphisms have $k$-morphisms for $k \leq n$.
\item $k$-morphisms for $k > n$ are all invertible (equivalences).
\end{itemize}

Models include: quasi-categories, Segal $n$-categories, complete Segal spaces, $\Theta_n$-spaces. Equivalence of these models is established by Ara--Maltsiniotis~\cite{AraMaltsiniotis2017}.

No unbounded-depth stabilization follows from these finite-depth comparisons. In particular, an expression such as $(\infty,\infty+1)$ requires a chosen meaning, and an equivalence with $(\infty,\infty)$ cannot be obtained merely by applying fixed-finite-$n$ unicity repeatedly; see Theorems~\ref{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity} and~\ref{thm:cond-infty-stab} and Remark~\ref{thm:bergner-lurie-stab}.

Reference: Lurie HTT~\cite{LurieHTT} for $(\infty, 1)$-categories; Riehl--Verity~\cite{RiehlVerity2022} for synthetic $(\infty, 1)$-category theory.

\subsection{Accessible Functors}

A functor $F : \cC \to \cD$ between locally small categories is \textbf{$\lambda$-accessible} (for a regular cardinal $\lambda$) if $F$ preserves $\lambda$-filtered colimits.

A category $\cC$ is \textbf{locally $\lambda$-presentable} if it is cocomplete and has a small dense subcategory of $\lambda$-presentable objects.

Reference: Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994} for accessibility theory.

\begin{lemma}[Lair--Makkai--Par\'e representation]\label{lem:lair}
Every accessible category is $2$-categorically equivalent to the $2$-category of models of a small sketch; equivalently, to the $2$-category of models of an accessible $2$-theory.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\emph{Reference for the proof.} Lair~\cite{Lair1981} ($1$-categorical sketches, original French source); Makkai--Par\'e~\cite{MakkaiPare1989} Chapter~3 (systematic development, including the $2$-categorical sketch/theory correspondence); Ad\'amek--Rosick\'y~\cite{AdamekRosicky1994} Chapter~2 (accessibility-theoretic treatment).  The lemma is used in this paper only through these references.

\subsection{Display Map Categories}

\begin{definition}[Display map category, $1$-categorical version]\label{def:display-1cat}
A \textbf{display map category} is a pair $(\cC, \cD)$ where $\cC$ is a category with pullbacks and $\cD \subseteq \Mor(\cC)$ is a class of morphisms stable under pullback and containing isomorphisms.
\end{definition}

Jacobs~\cite{Jacobs1999} establishes that display map categories provide categorical semantics for intensional type theories, with display morphisms corresponding to dependent type families.

Gambino--Garner~\cite{GambinoGarner2008} extends this to the $2$-categorical setting, yielding Definitions~\ref{def:display-class} and~\ref{def:display-2-cat}.

\subsection{Bicategory of Fractions}

\begin{lemma}[Pronk's bicategory of fractions]\label{lem:pronk}
Given a bicategory (in particular a $2$-category) $\cC$ and a class $\mathcal{W}$ of $1$-morphisms satisfying Pronk's right calculus-of-fractions conditions (BF1)--(BF5), the bicategory of fractions $\cC[\mathcal{W}^{-1}]$ exists and satisfies the universal property of $2$-localization at $\mathcal{W}$ (modulo size conditions when $\cC$ is large).
\end{lemma}

Reference: Pronk~\cite{Pronk1996} (\S 2.1, Thm.~21).

Pronk's conditions (BF1)--(BF5) are:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(BF1)}] \emph{Equivalences}: every internal equivalence of $\cC$ (in particular every identity $1$-morphism) belongs to $\mathcal{W}$.
\item[\textbf{(BF2)}] \emph{Composition}: $\mathcal{W}$ is closed under composition of $1$-morphisms.
\item[\textbf{(BF3)}] \emph{Right Ore condition for $1$-cells}: for every $w : A \to C$ in $\mathcal{W}$ and every $f : B \to C$ in $\cC$, there exists a span $A \xleftarrow{g} D \xrightarrow{v} B$ with $v \in \mathcal{W}$ and an invertible $2$-cell $\alpha : w \circ g \xRightarrow{\simeq} f \circ v$.
\item[\textbf{(BF4)}] \emph{Right cancellation / equalization for $2$-cells}: if $\alpha : w \circ f \Rightarrow w \circ g$ is a $2$-cell with $w \in \mathcal{W}$ and $f, g : A \to B$, then there exist $v : A' \to A$ in $\mathcal{W}$ and a $2$-cell $\beta : f \circ v \Rightarrow g \circ v$ such that $w \ast \beta = \alpha \ast v$; moreover, $\beta$ can be chosen invertible whenever $\alpha$ is invertible, and if $\beta, \beta' : f \circ v \Rightarrow g \circ v$ satisfy $w \ast \beta = w \ast \beta'$, there exists $u : A'' \to A'$ in $\mathcal{W}$ with $\beta \ast u = \beta' \ast u$.
\item[\textbf{(BF5)}] \emph{Saturation under invertible $2$-cells}: if $w \in \mathcal{W}$ and there is an invertible $2$-cell $w \xRightarrow{\simeq} w'$, then $w' \in \mathcal{W}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lawvere Fixed-Point Theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Lawvere fixed-point, $2$-categorical]\label{lem:lawvere-2}
In a $2$-cartesian closed category $\cC$ with a weakly point-surjective $1$-morphism $e : A \to Y^A$, every $f : Y \to Y$ has a fixed point: there exists $y_0 : 1 \to Y$ with $f \circ y_0 = y_0$ up to coherent $2$-equivalence.
\end{lemma}

Reference: Lawvere~\cite{Lawvere1969}; Yanofsky~\cite{Yanofsky2003} for $2$-categorical formulation.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional Lawvere result in a homotopy category]\label{lem:lawvere-inf}
Let a chosen higher-categorical model have a cartesian closed homotopy category $h\cC$. Suppose $e:A\to Y^A$ is weakly point-surjective in $h\cC$. Then every endomorphism $f:Y\to Y$ has a fixed global point in $h\cC$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the ordinary categorical diagonal argument in $h\cC$: represent the map $a\mapsto f(e(a)(a))$ by a point $a_0$ and evaluate at $a_0$. The resulting point $y_0=e(a_0)(a_0)$ satisfies $f(y_0)=y_0$ in $h\cC$.
\end{proof}
This conclusion is an equality in the homotopy category. A stronger coherent higher fixed-point structure requires a definition and further data. Finite-depth unicity does not construct a compatible family of fixed points or its limit.

\subsection{Whitehead-Type Criterion}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional reconstruction from a tower]\label{lem:whitehead}
Suppose the specified model reconstructs $\cC_i$ as the limit of a tower
$\cC_i^{(n)}$, and a functor $\Phi$ is the limit of a compatible family
of equivalences $\Phi_n:\cC_1^{(n)}\to\cC_2^{(n)}$. Then $\Phi$ is an
equivalence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Limits preserve equivalences of diagrams. The reconstruction and
compatibility hypotheses supply the indicated diagrams and their limits.
\end{proof}
Accessibility alone does not establish reconstruction from truncations.
Applications must verify this additional completeness property in their
chosen higher-categorical model.

\subsection{Enrichment Non-Commutativity and Conditional \texorpdfstring{$(\infty, \omega)$}{(infinity, omega)}-Stabilization}

Let $\mathcal{V}_n := (\infty, n)\text{-}\Cat$ (fixed up to equivalence for finite $n \in \mathbb{N}$ by Barwick--Schommer-Pries~\cite{BarwickSchommerPries2021}), and let $\iota_n : \mathcal{V}_n \hookrightarrow \mathcal{V}_{n+1}$ be the full embedding of $(\infty, n+1)$-categories whose $(n+1)$-morphisms are equivalences. Then $\iota_n$ admits both adjoints $\tau_{\le n} \dashv \iota_n \dashv \mathrm{core}_n$ (localization inverting $(n+1)$-morphisms on the left; maximal sub-$(\infty, n)$-category discarding non-invertible $(n+1)$-cells on the right), so $\iota_n$ preserves all small limits and colimits (and finite products). In $\widehat{\Cat}_\infty$, define the bounded-depth colimit $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}} := \varinjlim_{n < \omega}^{\widehat{\Cat}_\infty} (\mathcal{V}_n, \iota_n)$, the coinductive limit $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{coind}} := \varprojlim_{n < \omega}^{\widehat{\Cat}_\infty} (\mathcal{V}_n, \mathrm{core}_n) \simeq \varinjlim_{n < \omega}^{\mathrm{Pr}^L} (\mathcal{V}_n, \iota_n)$, and the truncation limit $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\tau\text{-}\mathrm{lim}} := \varprojlim_{n < \omega}^{\widehat{\Cat}_\infty} (\mathcal{V}_n, \tau_{\le n})$.

\begin{theorem}[Model separation and enrichment non-commutativity]\label{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity}
Let $\mathbb{D}_k \in \mathcal{V}_k$ denote the free walking non-invertible $k$-cell ($k \ge 1$), whose unique generating $k$-cell $\alpha_k$ is non-invertible.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Strict model separation}: $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}} \not\simeq \Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{coind}}$ and $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}} \not\simeq \Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\tau\text{-}\mathrm{lim}}$. Specifically, $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{coind}}$ and $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\tau\text{-}\mathrm{lim}}$ are presentable and cocomplete, whereas $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$ fails to admit the countable coproduct $\coprod_{k \ge 1} \mathbb{D}_k$.
\item \emph{Enrichment non-commutativity}: identifying $(\infty, n+1)\text{-}\Cat \simeq \Cat(\mathcal{V}_n)$ via $(\infty,1)$-enrichment (Gepner--Haugseng~\cite{GepnerHaugseng2015}), the canonical comparison functor
\[
  \Phi : \varinjlim_{n < \omega}^{\widehat{\Cat}_\infty} \Cat(\mathcal{V}_n) \longrightarrow \Cat\bigl(\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}\bigr)
\]
is fully faithful and \emph{not} essentially surjective.
\item \emph{Exact commutation on uniformly depth-bounded categories}: let $\Cat^{\mathrm{bdd\text{-}depth}}(\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}) \subseteq \Cat(\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}})$ be the full subcategory of enriched categories $\cC$ admitting a single $N < \omega$ with $\Hom_{\cC}(x, y) \in \mathcal{V}_N$ for all $x, y \in \Ob(\cC)$ (automatically satisfied when $\Ob(\cC)$ is finite). Then $\Phi$ restricts to an equivalence
\[
  \varinjlim_{n < \omega}^{\widehat{\Cat}_\infty} \Cat(\mathcal{V}_n) \;\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}\; \Cat^{\mathrm{bdd\text{-}depth}}\bigl(\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}\bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (i), each $\mathcal{V}_n$ is presentable, $\mathrm{core}_n$ is a right adjoint (in $\mathrm{Pr}^R$), and $\tau_{\le n}$ is a left adjoint (in $\mathrm{Pr}^L$). By Lurie HTT~\cite{LurieHTT} (Prop.~5.5.3.13, Cor.~5.5.3.4), both $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{coind}}$ and $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\tau\text{-}\mathrm{lim}}$ are presentable and hence admit all countable coproducts. Suppose a coproduct $C = \coprod_{k \ge 1} \mathbb{D}_k$ existed in $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$; then $C$ would lie in the essential image of $\mathcal{V}_N$ for some finite $N < \omega$. For the canonical injection $\mathrm{in}_{N+1} : \mathbb{D}_{N+1} \to C$, every $(N+1)$-cell of $C$ is an equivalence and functors preserve equivalences, so $\mathrm{in}_{N+1}(\alpha_{N+1})$ is an equivalence in $C$. By the coproduct universal property, the family $(g_k : \mathbb{D}_k \to \mathbb{D}_{N+1})_{k \ge 1}$ with $g_{N+1} = \id_{\mathbb{D}_{N+1}}$ would factor through $C$ via some $f : C \to \mathbb{D}_{N+1}$, forcing $\alpha_{N+1} \simeq f(\mathrm{in}_{N+1}(\alpha_{N+1}))$ to be an equivalence in $\mathbb{D}_{N+1}$, a contradiction. Hence $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$ is not equivalent to either limit category.

For (ii), each $\iota_n$ is fully faithful and preserves finite products, so $\Cat(\iota_n)$ is fully faithful~\cite{GepnerHaugseng2015} and the filtered colimit comparison $\Phi$ is fully faithful. To see that $\Phi$ is not essentially surjective, define $\mathcal{X} \in \Cat(\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}})$ with $\Ob(\mathcal{X}) := \mathbb{N}$ and hom-objects $\Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(0, k) := \mathbb{D}_k$ ($k \ge 1$), $\Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(i, i) := *$, and $\emptyset$ otherwise (trivial composition). If $\mathcal{X}$ were equivalent to some $\mathcal{Y} \in \Cat(\mathcal{V}_N)$ for finite $N$, then $\mathbb{D}_{N+1} = \Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(0, N+1)$ would be equivalent in $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$ to an object of $\mathcal{V}_N$, contradicting non-invertibility of $\alpha_{N+1}$.

For (iii), $\Phi$ is fully faithful as in (ii), and a $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$-enriched category lies in the essential image of $\varinjlim_{n < \omega} \Cat(\mathcal{V}_n)$ if and only if all of its hom-objects lie in a single $\mathcal{V}_N$ for some finite $N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-depth sufficiency and conditional $(\infty, \omega)$-stabilization]\label{thm:cond-infty-stab}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Finite-$n$ stability of AFN-T witnesses}: for every $S \in \RS$ whose syntactic category $\Syn(S)$ has finite non-invertible depth $n_S < \omega$, the truncation tower $\{\tau_{\le k}(\Syn(S))\}_{k < \omega}$ stabilizes identically at $k = n_S$ ($\iota_{n_S}(\Syn(S)) \simeq \Syn(S)$ in every $\mathcal{V}_k$ for $k \ge n_S$). Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:vertical} holds at finite $n_S < \omega$ without requiring $n = \infty$ stabilization.
\item[\textup{(ii-a)}] \emph{Bounded-depth sub-doctrine}: fix $\mathcal{M}_\omega \in \{\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{coind}}, \Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\tau\text{-}\mathrm{lim}}\}$. Restricting to uniformly depth-bounded enriched categories (every hom-object in a single $\mathcal{V}_N$, $N < \omega$) gives $\Cat^{\mathrm{bdd\text{-}depth}}(\mathcal{M}_\omega) \simeq \Cat^{\mathrm{bdd\text{-}depth}}(\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}) \simeq \Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity}~(iii); this stabilizes only the strictly smaller bounded-depth sub-doctrine $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}} \not\simeq \mathcal{M}_\omega$, not $\mathcal{M}_\omega$ itself.
\item[\textup{(ii-b)}] \emph{Conditional full $(\infty, \omega)$-stabilization}: for $\mathcal{M}_\omega \in \{\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{coind}}, \Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\tau\text{-}\mathrm{lim}}\}$ with defining tower $\mathcal{M}_\omega = \varprojlim_{n < \omega} (\mathcal{V}_n, q_n)$ ($q_n \in \{\mathrm{core}_n, \tau_{\le n}\}$), full enrichment stabilization $\Cat(\mathcal{M}_\omega) \simeq \mathcal{M}_\omega$ holds conditionally under \textup{(Stab-1)} Postnikov/Whitehead tower reconstruction in $\mathcal{M}_\omega$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:whitehead}) and \textup{(Stab-2')} limit-commutation $\Cat(\varprojlim_{n < \omega} \mathcal{V}_n) \simeq \varprojlim_{n < \omega} \Cat(\mathcal{V}_n)$ compatibly with $\Cat(\mathcal{V}_n) \simeq \mathcal{V}_{n+1}$ and the transition maps $q_n$. Unconditional enrichment stabilization for arbitrary unbounded $(\infty, \omega)$-categories remains open.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Part (i) holds because every $k$-morphism of $\Syn(S)$ for $k > n_S$ is already invertible, placing $\Syn(S)$ in the essential image of $\mathcal{V}_{n_S} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{V}_k$ for all $k \ge n_S$. For (ii-a), every object of $\Cat^{\mathrm{bdd\text{-}depth}}(\mathcal{M}_\omega)$ has hom-objects in a single $\mathcal{V}_N \hookrightarrow \Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{M}_\omega$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity}~(iii) gives $\Cat^{\mathrm{bdd\text{-}depth}}(\mathcal{M}_\omega) \simeq \Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$, strictly smaller than $\mathcal{M}_\omega$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity}~(i). For (ii-b), \textup{(Stab-1)} and \textup{(Stab-2')} yield $\Cat(\mathcal{M}_\omega) \simeq \varprojlim_{n < \omega} \Cat(\mathcal{V}_n) \simeq \varprojlim_{n < \omega} \mathcal{V}_{n+1} \simeq \mathcal{M}_\omega$ via Lemma~\ref{lem:whitehead}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No unbounded stabilization theorem supplied]\label{thm:bergner-lurie-stab}
Writing $\colim_{n<\omega}(\infty,n)\text{-}\Cat$ does not prove that
enrichment commutes with that colimit; indeed Theorem~\ref{thm:enrichment-noncommutativity} proves unconditionally that $\Phi$ is not essentially surjective and that $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{bdd}}$ is strictly separated from $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\mathrm{coind}}$ and $\Cat_{(\infty,\omega)}^{\tau\text{-}\mathrm{lim}}$. Barwick--Schommer-Pries~\cite{BarwickSchommerPries2021} establish unicity
for fixed finite $n$, with space of theories $B(\mathbb Z/2)^n$.
Their result supplies neither general unbounded interchange nor an equivalence between
successive moduli spaces of foundations; unconditional $(\infty, \omega)$-stabilization beyond the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cond-infty-stab} remains open.
\end{remark}

\section{Paraconsistent Extension}\label{app:paraconsistent}

Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} applies to Rich-metatheories in $\RS$. This appendix formulates a conditional transfer to paraconsistent Rich-metatheories equipped with an explicitly specified classical kernel and a boundary-predicate-preserving translation (strongly dialetheic theories lacking such a translation are not covered).

\subsection{Paraconsistent R-S}

\begin{definition}[Paraconsistent R-S with extractable classical kernel]\label{def:paraconsistent-rs}
A theory $S'$ is a \textbf{paraconsistent Rich-metatheory with extractable classical kernel} if it satisfies the conditions (R1')--(R5') analogous to (R1)--(R5), together with:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Explosion-min)}: there exists a formula $\phi$ such that $\bot \not\vdash_{S'} \phi$ (equivalently, $\neg\forall\phi\,(\bot \vdash_{S'} \phi)$: contradictions do not entail every formula).
\item \textbf{(Strong-neg)}: there exists a negation $\sim$ such that $(p \wedge \sim p) \vdash_\sim \bot$ (a specific contradiction principle, though not full explosion).
\item \textbf{(Classical-kernel extractability)}: an explicitly specified classical fragment $S^*$ is consistent and belongs to $\RS$. Preservation of candidate definitions, interpretations, and boundary predicates under a translation from $S'$ is additional data, as required by Theorem~\ref{thm:paraconsistent-afnt}. No largest such fragment is asserted to exist automatically.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Paraconsistent AFN-T}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional transfer to a paraconsistent presentation]\label{thm:paraconsistent-afnt}
Suppose a specified classical Rich-metatheory $S^*$ and a translation from $S'$ to $S^*$ are given. Assume that this translation sends every candidate satisfying all three $\LAbs$ conditions in $S'$ to a candidate satisfying all three conditions in $S^*$ within the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha}. Then no such candidate exists in $S'$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (sketch)]
A candidate in $S'$ would translate to a candidate in $S^*$, contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:afnt-alpha} there.
\end{proof}
Neither a strong negation nor the name ``classical kernel'' constructs this translation. Consistency and Rich-membership of a kernel, functoriality of the translation, and preservation of $\Pi_4$ and $\Pi_{3\text{-max}}$ remain separate obligations. Removing negation symbols is not a semantics-preserving translation.
